# Academic literature on representing multiple perspectives in shared knowledge systems

Scope: (a) empirical NPOV/conflict research on Wikipedia; (b) knowledge representation with multiple contexts/viewpoints; (c) HCI/CSCW/hypertext prototypes for multi-perspective or "contested" knowledge; (d) LLM-era perspective-aware summarization, pluralistic alignment, bridging, and AI mediation; (e) historical design precedents for several labeled voices on one page. Compiled 2026-09-21. Every item gives the mechanism and the finding; "unverified" flags anything I could not open a primary source for.

## Q1. Wikipedia's NPOV and its conflicts: is one neutral article achievable, and what alternatives were proposed?

### Takeaway
The empirical record says NPOV works as a *behavioral* rule that lets strangers collaborate and does measurably push articles toward neutrality with enough revisions and editors — but it converges only where many editors show up, its meaning is settled by deliberate ambiguity rather than definition, a residual class of "never-ending war" articles never converges, and the sanctioned Wikipedia answer to that residue is still one article (POV forks are banned). The alternatives actually proposed — Sanger's expert review, Wikinfo's "sympathetic point of view" with per-POV articles, forking as exit, and cross-language comparison tools — all move plurality *outside* the single article rather than into it.

### Cited Findings

**What NPOV is (policy text and its genealogy)**
- Wikipedia's policy defines NPOV as "representing fairly, proportionately, and, as far as possible, without editorial bias, all the significant views that have been published by reliable sources on a topic"; it requires "facts about opinions" rather than opinions stated as facts, "due weight" proportional to prominence in reliable sources, warns against "false balance", and declares itself "non-negotiable ... [not] superseded by other policies or guidelines, nor by editor consensus." — [Wikipedia:Neutral point of view](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Neutral_point_of_view)
- Reagle (*Good Faith Collaboration*, MIT Press 2010, ch. 3) reads NPOV as an epistemic *stance* for collaborators rather than a claim about content: "assert facts, including facts about opinions—but do not assert the opinions themselves"; it operationalizes through "writing for the enemy" (explain the other side so its adherents feel "understood") and "ideological anonymity" (readers can't tell the author's leaning). Wales is quoted: NPOV "was this idea of a social concept, for helping people get along." Reagle also records "Gizza's First Law" — respect for all verifiable perspectives is not *equal coverage*; conflating the two marks a troll. He concedes "neutral" cannot be defined without equally "problematic" terms (unbiased, fair, impartial) and treats it as a practical, not metaphysical, stance. — [Reagle, ch. 3](https://reagle.org/joseph/2010/gfc/chapter-3.html); summary of the book's argument that "NPOV renders the subject matter of a collaborative encyclopedia compatible, good faith makes it possible to work together" — [IJoC review](https://ijoc.org/index.php/ijoc/article/download/2542/1036/10040)
- Matei & Dobrescu, "Wikipedia's 'Neutral Point of View': Settling Conflict through Ambiguity," *The Information Society* 27(1):40–51, 2011: analyzing NPOV's constitutive documents and talk-page discussion, they conclude "ambiguity is at the heart of the policy process on Wikipedia," that it "is not extraneous, but a central ingredient," that it "naturally develops from the pluralist and non-hierarchic values" of the culture, and that NPOV is "frequently used as a justification for personal biases" — so "neutrality" as practiced must be reconsidered. — [Matei summary](http://matei.org/ithink/ambiguity-conflict-wikipedia/); [publisher](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/01972243.2011.534368)

**Conflict measured**
- Kittur, Suh, Pendleton & Chi, "He Says, She Says: Conflict and Coordination in Wikipedia," CHI 2007, pp. 453–462. Mechanism: defines "direct work" (article edits) vs "indirect work" (talk, user, policy, maintenance); a *Controversial Revision Count* (CRC) per article; an SMOreg model predicting CRC from page metrics; and a *Revert Graph* tool visualizing who reverts whom. Findings: article edits fell from ~90% of all edits in 2001 to ~70% by July 2006; edits to Wikipedia-namespace pages rose from ~2% to ~12%; ~3.9M reverts detected (MD5 + comment methods), ~6.7% of work goes to restoring articles; the model reaches R² = 0.897 on tagged-controversial articles and correlates with admin conflict ratings on untagged ones (r = .47). Top predictor is talk-page revision count; **more unique editors *negatively* correlates with conflict** ("having more points of view can defuse conflict"); anonymous edits raise conflict on talk pages but lower it on article pages. The authors flag "balancing the desire to reduce controversies and the need to provide protection for minority opinions expressed through anonymity" as an open design question. — [PDF](https://gwern.net/doc/wikipedia/2007-kittur.pdf); [ACM](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/1240624.1240698)
- Yasseri, Sumi, Rung, Kornai & Kertész, "Dynamics of Conflicts in Wikipedia," *PLoS ONE* 7(6):e38869, 2012. Mechanism: controversy measure M = E × Σ over mutually-reverting editor pairs of min(N_i^d, N_j^r) (E = number of editors ever in a mutual revert; N = the pair's edit counts), i.e. weight reverts by how established the fighters are. Findings: fewer than 1% of pages are controversial (~12k of 3.2M with M ≥ 10³); three long-run patterns — (a) *consensus* (M grows then saturates; Gompertz fit R² > 0.95), (b) *sequence of temporary consensuses* (war/peace cycles, endogenously driven by "a constant influx of new editors, who are not satisfied with the previously settled state"; mean spacing ≈ 1300 ± 90 edits), (c) *never-ending wars* where "no permanent, or even temporary, consensus gets ever built"; "there is hope for consensus by natural process as long as M < 10⁶." Edit wars are fought by few: the top 5 reverting pairs account for > 0.5 of M for most articles; only never-ending-war articles show "a steady flow of replacement armies." Conclusion: "even in the controversial articles, often a consensus can be achieved in a reasonable time, and ... those articles which do not achieve consensus are driven by an influx of newly arriving editors and external events." — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1202.3643); [PLoS ONE](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article/file?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0038869&type=printable)

**Slant measured (Greenstein & Zhu programme)**
- Greenstein & Zhu, "Open Content, Linus' Law, and Neutral Point of View," *Information Systems Research* 27(3):618–635, 2016. Mechanism: Gentzkow–Shapiro congressional-phrase slant index applied to Wikipedia U.S.-politics articles over their revision histories. Findings: early political entries "leaned Democrat and tended to be biased, but both traits diminished over time"; support only for a *narrow* Linus' Law — more contributors makes an article more neutral — with no evidence for a broad version, because "the majority of articles receive little attention, and most articles change only mildly from their initial slant." — [INFORMS](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/isre.2016.0643); earlier NBER version "Collective Intelligence and Neutral Point of View: The Case of Wikipedia," w18167, 2012 — [NBER](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w18167/w18167.pdf)
- Greenstein & Zhu, "Do Experts or Crowd-Based Models Produce More Bias? Evidence from Encyclopædia Britannica and Wikipedia," *MIS Quarterly* 42(3), 2018: on matched U.S.-politics article pairs, "Wikipedia articles are more slanted toward Democratic views than are Britannica articles, as well as more biased"; "the difference in bias between a pair of articles decreases with more revisions"; "the bias on a per word basis hardly differs between the sources because Wikipedia articles tend to be longer." — [AIS abstract](https://aisel.aisnet.org/misq/vol42/iss3/14/); sample of 3,918 matched pairs (73% identical titles) reported via — [Harvard DASH PDF](https://dash.harvard.edu/bitstream/handle/1/41946110/greenstein,zhu_do-experts-or-crowd.pdf?sequence=1&isAllowed=y)
- Pavalanathan, Han & Eisenstein, "Mind Your POV: Convergence of Articles and Editors Towards Wikipedia's Neutrality Norm," CSCW 2018: interrupted time series around NPOV tags shows "a significant decrease in biased language in the article" after tagging, but "NPOV corrections and talk page discussions yield no significant change" in the tagged *editors'* subsequent word usage — articles converge, editors don't enculturate. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1809.06951)

**Bias as language (NLP on NPOV edits)**
- Recasens, Danescu-Niculescu-Mizil & Jurafsky, "Linguistic Models for Analyzing and Detecting Biased Language," ACL 2013: from real NPOV-removal edits they distinguish *framing bias* (subjective/perspective-specific words) from *epistemological bias* (presupposed or entailed propositions treated as uncontroversial). — [ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/P13-1162/)
- Hube & Fetahu, "Detecting Biased Statements in Wikipedia," WWW 2018 Companion: word2vec-expanded bias lexicon + syntactic/LIWC/framing/epistemological features, crowdsourced labels; 74% accuracy. — [ACM](https://dl.acm.org/doi/fullHtml/10.1145/3184558.3191640)
- Pryzant, Diehl Martinez, Dass, Kurohashi, Jurafsky & Yang, "Automatically Neutralizing Subjective Bias in Text," AAAI 2020: first parallel corpus of 180,000 biased→neutral sentence pairs from Wikipedia NPOV edits; BERT tagger + join-embedding editor. — [Stanford PDF](https://nlp.stanford.edu/pubs/pryzant2020bias.pdf)
- Ashkinaze, Guan, Kurek, Adar, Budak & Gilbert, "Seeing Like an AI: How LLMs Apply (and Misapply) Wikipedia Neutrality Norms," ICWSM 2026: LLMs detect NPOV violations at only 64% on a balanced set but remove 79% of the words editors removed while making "additional changes beyond Wikipedia editors' simpler neutralizations"; crowdworkers nonetheless preferred AI rewrites as more neutral (70%) and fluent (61%). — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.04183)
- Johnson, Liou, Rogers, Shaw & Zia (Wikimedia Foundation), "Recommended Practices for NPOV Research on Wikipedia," arXiv 2510.21526, Oct 2025: a white paper noting "surprisingly little rigorous scholarly attention" to NPOV and giving methodological guidance. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.21526)

**Language editions as de facto multiple perspectives**
- Hecht & Gergle, "The Tower of Babel Meets Web 2.0," CHI 2010: across 25 language editions, both *which* concepts are included and *how* they are described diverge substantially; builds on their 2009 "self-focus bias" measure. — [CollabLab PDF](https://collablab.northwestern.edu/pubs/CHI2010-HechtAndGergle-TowerOfBabel.pdf)
- Bao, Hecht et al., "Omnipedia: Bridging the Wikipedia Language Gap," CHI 2012: a UI that aligns 25 editions' articles on a topic and shows which facts are unique to which edition; users "strategically compared how language editions defined concepts." — [PDF](https://collablab.northwestern.edu/pubs/CHI2012-BaoEtAl-Omnipedia.pdf)
- Massa & Scrinzi, "Manypedia: Comparing Language Points of View of Wikipedia Communities," WikiSym 2012 / *First Monday* 18(1), 2013: side-by-side two-edition view with machine translation, edit/editor stats, top-5 editors, and a "concept similarity" score from outbound-link overlap; coins *Linguistic Point of View* (LPOV). Examples: Greek WP calls Northern Cyprus "under Turkish occupation since 1974 in violation of international legal norms," Turkish WP "an independent state." — [First Monday](https://firstmonday.org/ojs/index.php/fm/article/download/3939/3382)
- Borra et al., "Societal Controversies in Wikipedia Articles" / Contropedia, CHI 2015: a language-agnostic edit-history measure over wiki-links identifies *which topics within an article* were most controversial and when, and overlays that on the article. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1904.08721); [contropedia.net](https://contropedia.net/)
- Wang, Zhang, Yoon, Vincent, Samir & Shwartz, "WikiGap," arXiv 2505.24195, 2025: surfaces facts present in French/Russian/Chinese editions but absent from English inside the English article; 21-participant study, faster/more accurate fact finding, +32 usability points over interlanguage links, and users "reconsidered the completeness of English Wikipedia." — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.24195)

**Alternatives proposed**
- Sanger, "Why Wikipedia Must Jettison Its Anti-Elitism," Kuro5hin, 31 Dec 2004: two problems — low perceived credibility and "the dominance of difficult people, trolls, and their enablers" — rooted in "lack of respect for expertise"; remedies: an official expertise policy, or an expert-vetted fork (the seed of Citizendium). — [larrysanger.org](https://larrysanger.org/2004/12/why-wikipedia-must-jettison-its-anti-elitism/)
- Sanger, "Wikipedia Is Badly Biased," May 2020: claims Wikipedia abandoned the original NPOV (an article "written not to take sides" such that readers can't "detect the editors' political alignment"); proposes a decentralized "Encyclosphere" network of independent encyclopedias, or that Wikipedia "come clean" with a credibility policy instead of claiming neutrality. — [larrysanger.org](https://larrysanger.org/2020/05/wikipedia-is-badly-biased/). His later nine-point plan includes "enabling competing articles" — [SAN report](https://san.com/cc/wikipedia-co-founder-says-site-has-liberal-bias-heres-his-plan-to-fix-that/); his "WikiProject Intellectual Diversity" proposal ended with an indefinite ban for off-wiki canvassing — [Jewish Chronicle](https://www.thejc.com/news/world/wikipedia-bans-co-founder-anti-bias-initiative-xcqac2ig)
- Wikinfo (fork, 2003): "the main article on each subject ... adopts a 'sympathetic point of view', and points of view can be added, usually as articles linked to the main article" — i.e. per-POV articles clustered around a topic instead of one NPOV article. — [Wikinfo about](https://www.wikinfo.org/w/index-php/wikinfo_about.html); [History of wikis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_wikis)
- Wikipedia's own rule against this: a POV fork is "a content fork deliberately created to avoid a neutral point of view"; "all facts and major points of view on a certain subject should be treated in one article"; splits "are permissible only if written from a neutral point of view" and "must not be an attempt to evade the consensus process" (summary-style spinouts only). — [Wikipedia:Content forking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Content_forking)
- Tkacz, "The Politics of Forking Paths," in Lovink & Tkacz (eds.), *Critical Point of View: A Wikipedia Reader*, Institute of Network Cultures, 2011, pp. 94–109: reads the 2002 Spanish fork as "exit-oriented political activity" analogous to exodus/revolution. — [WRAP](http://wrap.warwick.ac.uk/53293/). His interview with fork leader Edgar Enyedy: grievances were Bomis's server/domain control, stale software for non-English editions, the advertising threat, and treating other languages as translations; Enyedy: "The project itself was not intended to last. It was merely a form of pressure." — [CPOV](https://networkcultures.org/cpov/2011/01/15/spanish_fork/)
- Tkacz, *Wikipedia and the Politics of Openness*, U. Chicago Press 2015: forking is the exit valve that "openness" promises, but openness "obscures its own inner workings, sweeping power differentials and inequality under an apolitical rug" — consensus + fork is not a neutral procedure. — [U. Chicago Press](https://press.uchicago.edu/ucp/books/book/chicago/W/bo19085555.html); [THE review](https://www.timeshighereducation.com/books/wikipedia-and-the-politics-of-openness-by-nathaniel-tkacz/2017640.article)

### Inferences
- Three separate literatures agree on one shape: neutrality is a *convergence process* that needs many editors (Kittur: unique editors reduce conflict; Greenstein & Zhu: narrow Linus' Law) and stalls where attention is thin or fresh partisans keep arriving (Yasseri's category (c)). A design that labels perspectives explicitly is addressing exactly the residue those processes leave — hot, high-M topics and low-traffic articles — not the median article.
- Matei & Dobrescu plus Reagle imply NPOV's *ambiguity* is load-bearing: a wiki that hard-codes perspective labels removes the ambiguity that lets Wikipedians keep cooperating, so it needs a replacement social mechanism for "what counts as a perspective" and "who may speak for it".
- Every actually-built alternative (Wikinfo, Conservapedia, Citizendium, Encyclosphere, the language editions themselves) put plurality *between* articles or sites; the tools that succeeded with users (Omnipedia, Manypedia, WikiGap, Contropedia) put plurality *beside* one article as a comparison view. Nothing in this corpus reports a deployed wiki with several labeled POVs *inside* one article page.
- Recasens' framing-vs-epistemological split is a usable editorial rule for a multi-POV article: perspective sections may carry framing bias by design; the shared factual spine must not carry epistemological bias.

### Gaps
- Greenstein & Zhu's AER P&P 2012 note "Is Wikipedia Biased?" and Greenstein, Gu & Zhu's *Management Science* paper on editor ideology were not opened; cite from the 2016/2018 papers above instead.
- I could not open Tkacz's 2015 chapter text; the forking/exit characterization is from the 2011 essay abstract, the publisher blurb, and reviews.
- Wikinfo's launch date and current status are from secondary pages; the "sympathetic point of view" quote is from Wikinfo's own about page.

## Q2. Knowledge representation with multiple contexts/viewpoints in one knowledge base

### Takeaway
KR has three mature answers, each isolating contradiction at a different grain: *contexts as first-class objects* (Cyc microtheories, McCarthy/Guha `ist`, CKR), *per-perspective partial specifications with tolerated inconsistency and explicit inter-view rules* (ViewPoints), and *per-statement provenance* (named graphs, Wikidata claims with references, qualifiers and ranks). Wikidata is the only one running at wiki scale, and its rule is instructive: contradictory sourced claims may coexist, but ranks record *consensus*, never a contributor's side.

### Cited Findings
- **Cyc microtheories.** Each microtheory is "an internally consistent collection of facts about a particular domain"; different ones "may include incompatible facts. Instead of trying to hold the whole knowledge base in synchrony, we can have independent microtheories floating, each of which is internally consistent." — [Yuret, Cyc notes](https://www2.denizyuret.com/pub/cyc96/node17.html). "Unlike the knowledge base as a whole, each microtheory must be free from monotonic contradictions"; "each microtheory is a first-class object in the Cyc ontology; it has a name that is a regular constant." — [Wikipedia: Cyc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyc). The Companion cognitive architecture restates the design (Guha 1992; Lenat 1998): "bundles of assertions that are logically consistent and belong in the same reasoning context ... there can be contradictions among them." — [arXiv 2407.06401](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2407.06401). Formal roots: Guha, *Contexts: A Formalization and Some Applications*, Stanford PhD thesis / MCC ACT-CYC-423-91, 1991 — [Semantic Scholar](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/Contexts:-a-formalization-and-some-applications-Guha/63dc637595eec644f0462f87996fe90edc85c763); McCarthy, "Notes on Formalizing Context," IJCAI 1993, with the basic relation ist(c, p) — [IJCAI PDF](https://www.ijcai.org/Proceedings/93-1/Papers/078.pdf). Reported cost: "the copy and edit mechanisms to create and modify contexts turned out to be a bothersome source of logical inconsistencies ... as more contexts were created it became difficult and time consuming for the knowledge enterer to select the most adequate context for new assertions." — [Context in CYC (thesis)](https://www.unav.es/gep/TesisDoctorales/MarkGTesis.pdf)
- **ViewPoints (Finkelstein, Kramer, Nuseibeh, Finkelstein & Goedicke, IJSEKE 2(1):31–57, 1992).** A ViewPoint is a locally owned partial specification with five *slots*: a representation style, a domain, a specification, a work plan, and a work record; "the multiple perspectives problem" is "many actors, sundry representation schemes, diverse domain knowledge, differing development strategies." — [City Research Online PDF](https://openaccess.city.ac.uk/id/eprint/26496/1/ijseke92.pdf). Easterbrook, Finkelstein, Kramer & Nuseibeh, "Co-ordinating Conflicting ViewPoints by Managing Inconsistency," 1994: "maintaining global consistency at all times is an unnecessary burden ... it is often desirable to tolerate and even encourage inconsistency ... The focus therefore shifts from maintaining consistency to the management of inconsistencies"; "the framework tolerates inconsistency, with no requirement for changes to one [ViewPoint to propagate]"; consistency is defined only by explicit *inter-ViewPoint rules*, and they separate *inconsistency* (a state of the ViewPoint collection) from *conflict* and *mistake* (properties of owners' actions), using the work record to guide resolution. — [Toronto PDF](http://www.cs.toronto.edu/~sme/papers/1994/AID94_conflict_paper.pdf). The logic-based companion: Finkelstein, Gabbay, Hunter, Kramer & Nuseibeh, "Inconsistency Handling in Multi-Perspective Specifications," ESEC 1993 / IEEE TSE 1994. — [Springer](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/3-540-57209-0_7)
- **Multi-viewpoint ontologies.** Ribière & Dieng-Kuntz, "A Viewpoint Model for Cooperative Building of an Ontology," ICCS 2002 (C-VISTA / CG-VISTA, INRIA ACACIA): a viewpoint is "an interface allowing the indexation and the interpretation of a view composed of knowledge elements," implemented as extensions of conceptual graphs / description logics so several experts' models coexist. — [Springer](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/3-540-45483-7_17)
- **Contextual ontologies on the Semantic Web.** Bouquet et al., C-OWL, 2003 (contextual OWL with bridge rules); Serafini & Homola, "Contextualized Knowledge Repositories for the Semantic Web," *J. Web Semantics* 12, 2012: a CKR is "a set of OWL 2 knowledge bases, which are embedded in a context by a set of qualifying attributes (time, space, topic, etc.) specifying the boundaries within which the knowledge base is assumed to be true," with contexts in a hierarchical coverage relation for reuse. — [FBK CKR](https://dkm.fbk.eu/technologies/theoretical-frameworks/ckr-contextualized-knowledge-repository/); [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S157082681100103X)
- **Named graphs.** Carroll, Bizer, Hayes & Stickler, "Named Graphs, Provenance and Trust," WWW 2005: extends RDF syntax and semantics so a graph can be named and described by other statements — the mechanism for attaching provenance, and thus contested claims, at graph granularity; queryable via SPARQL. — [ACM](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/1060745.1060835)
- **Wikidata's plurality.** "Wikidata is not a database that stores *facts* about the world, but a secondary knowledge base that collects and links to references to such knowledge"; "Because statements essentially point to referenceable sources of information and different sources may provide contradicting information, it's possible to represent a plurality of perspectives on Wikidata"; "In case of disputes, community consensus determines the value of a property, however other points of views can be added as additional values as long as they include a source and appropriate qualifiers"; edit-warring over values is prohibited. — [Help:Statements](https://www.wikidata.org/wiki/Help:Statements/en). Ranks: *preferred* = "the most current statement or statements that best represent consensus (be it scientific consensus or the Wikidata community consensus)"; *normal* = default, "no judgement or evaluation"; *deprecated* = "known to include errors ... or that represent outdated knowledge"; **"Ranks are not a way of asserting your view for a disputed value, but instead are used for communicating the consensus opinion"**; qualifiers P7452 "reason for preferred rank," P2241 "reason for deprecated rank," P1310 "statement disputed by"; queries default to best-rank ("truthy") statements. — [Help:Ranking](https://www.wikidata.org/wiki/Help:Ranking). Vrandečić & Krötzsch, "Wikidata: A Free Collaborative Knowledgebase," *CACM* 57(10):78–85, 2014, is the design paper. — [ACM](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/2629489)
- **How Wikidata's plurality is used in practice.** Di Pasquale, Pasqual, Tomasi & Vitali, "On assessing weaker logical status claims in Wikidata cultural heritage records," *Semantic Web* 2024: four coexisting mechanisms for uncertain/competing/outdated claims — non-asserted statements, ranks, non-existing-value objects, and qualifiers (P5102 nature of statement, P1480 sourcing circumstances, P2241) — are rare in prevalence and "often ambiguous" in use; they recommend standardizing. — [SAGE](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.3233/SW-243686)
- **Polyvocality as a research agenda.** van Erp & de Boer, "A Polyvocal and Contextualised Semantic Web," ESWC 2021, LNCS 12731, pp. 506–512: KGs "reflect the popular or majority vote ... an inherent artifact of the frequency bias" of statistical KG construction; sets three challenges for representing multiple voices with context. — [Springer](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-030-77385-4_30). Vitali & Pasqual, "Provenance-Enhanced Statements in Knowledge Graphs," 2026: the DEC (Doxastic/Epistemic/Conjectural) framework reads provenance predicates as epistemic stance and sorts statements into "cognitive worlds" (beliefs isolated from facts; knowledge that permeates to reality; hypotheses), with locality of inference per world and controlled permeation, so disagreements and "delusions" don't collapse into inconsistency; prototype reasoner on Jena/Fuseki over named graphs, RDF-star and reification; cases: *Salvator Mundi* attribution, peptic-ulcer causation. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.15246). Their premise: "some [data] may be widely accepted, others contested, provisional, hypothetical, abandoned, illusory, or idiosyncratic. Knowledge graphs representing only a single view are losing relevant information as the divergence carries meaning and reflects the state of a debate." — same source

### Inferences
- The three grains map onto a multi-POV wiki directly: a *perspective* is a microtheory/named graph (a container asserted true only within its boundary), a per-perspective *article* is a ViewPoint (own style, domain, work plan, work record — with explicit cross-perspective rules for the shared facts), and individual *claims* carry Wikidata-style references and a consensus rank that is deliberately not a partisanship marker.
- Cyc's reported failure mode — contributors can't pick the right context, and copy-edit across contexts breeds inconsistency — is the concrete risk of letting perspectives multiply freely; ViewPoints' answer (tolerate inconsistency, but make every cross-view relationship an explicit checkable rule, and log the work record) is the mitigation.
- Wikidata's "ranks record consensus, disputes go to talk" is a precedent for separating *which view is mainstream* (a community judgment on the shared spine) from *which views exist* (any, if sourced and qualified).

### Gaps
- "Multi-perspective knowledge graph" as a search term returns KG-completion/embedding papers (e.g., MGIF, LREC 2024 "Multi-perspective Improvement of KGC with LLMs") that use "perspective" to mean feature views, not viewpoints; I found no paper under that phrase about contested claims. The relevant work is under "polyvocal", "contextualised", "weaker logical status", and "provenance-enhanced".
- I could not open the CACM Wikidata paper to quote its own wording on plurality; the quotations above are from Wikidata's Help pages.
- The ESEC 1993 / TSE 1994 inconsistency-handling paper's specific meta-rule formalism ("inconsistency implies action") was not opened; the tolerance stance is quoted from the 1994 Easterbrook et al. paper instead.

## Q3. HCI/CSCW/hypertext proposals and prototypes for multi-perspective, "contested", agonistic or pluralistic knowledge

### Takeaway
No CSCW/CHI/hypertext paper I could verify describes a deployed *wiki* carrying several labeled points of view per article; the nearest built systems are (i) comparison overlays on Wikipedia (Q1), (ii) *argument/claim-structured* systems that store stance as typed links rather than prose (Cohere/CCI, ConsiderIt, Deliberatorium, Kialo, Dispute Finder), (iii) opinion-mapping deliberation tools (Polis), and (iv) Everything2's multiple signed write-ups per node. The theory side (Mouffe via DiSalvo; epistemic-injustice critiques; multiperspectivity in history education) argues *for* designed dissensus but supplies no article-level mechanism.

### Cited Findings
- **Agonistic / adversarial design.** DiSalvo, *Adversarial Design*, MIT Press 2012, "translate[s] the ideas of agonistic pluralism (Mouffe) to interaction design": design should create "spaces of confrontation" and treat "contestation and dissensus as vital components of a democratic society." — [Goodreads description/reviews](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/16779743); [Google Books](https://books.google.com/books/about/Adversarial_Design.html?id=gdcGEAAAQBAJ). "A Critical Exploration of Agonistic Participatory Design," *The Design Journal* 2019: agonistic PD rests on Mouffe's view that "there is a need for agonistic spaces in which diverging views are allowed to emerge" to make power structures "available for debate and further investigation." — [Taylor & Francis](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/14606925.2019.1684730)
- **Contested Collective Intelligence (De Liddo & Buckingham Shum, CSCW 2010 workshop; De Liddo, Sándor & Buckingham Shum, *Computer Supported Cooperative Work* 21, 2012).** CCI is the subset of collective intelligence for settings with "different intellectual traditions, competing organizational objectives, information overload, or ambiguous environmental signals." Cohere, the prototype: ideas are nodes, connections are *typed* semantic links (IBIS roles — questions, answers, pros, cons — plus supports/challenges), sourced by social annotation of web documents, shown in map, connection-network (with authors) and timeline views; integrated with Xerox's XIP discourse parser so machine-detected contrast markers seed human annotation. — [CSCW 2010 PDF](https://oro.open.ac.uk/19554/1/DeLiddo-CSCW2010-CIorg-Final.pdf); [OU archive](https://research-archive.stem.open.ac.uk/cohere/); [Semantic Scholar](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/Contested-Collective-Intelligence%3A-Rationale%2C-and-a-Liddo-S%C3%A1ndor/3b8ceaf91b3e4b794d9536e1af32db1b8d839272)
- **ConsiderIt (Kriplean, Morgan, Freelon, Borning & Bennett, CSCW 2012).** Frames deliberation around pro/con points that participants "create, adopt, and share" into a personal stance; deployed on nine Washington ballot measures. — [ACM](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/2145204.2145249). **Reflect (Kriplean et al., CHI 2012)** adds "a space next to every comment where others can summarize the points they hear the commenter making" — restatement as a listening primitive. — [ACM](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/2207676.2208621)
- **Dispute Finder (Ennals, Trushkowsky, Agosta, Rattenbury & Hirsch, WWW 2010).** Browser extension that highlights text resembling known disputed claims and, on click, "shows them a list of articles that support other points of view"; the claim database is crawled from dispute lists and grown by users marking "This is disputed" / "Use as evidence." — [WWW 2010 PDF](https://www.ra.ethz.ch/cdstore/www2010/www/p341.pdf); [ACM](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/1772690.1772726)
- **MIT Deliberatorium (Klein, 2007–).** Large-scale argument mapping: issues → ideas → pro/con arguments in one deduplicated map, with moderation, rating and voting; used by "thousands of individuals" at Intel, the U.S. Bureau of Land Management and the Italian Democratic Party; shows argument-centric structure can work with groups > 150. — [Klein research page](https://cci.mit.edu/klein-old/research/); [Participedia case](https://participedia.net/case/4229)
- **Polis (Small, Bjorkegren, Erkkilä, Shaw & Megill, *RECERCA* 26(2), 2021).** Participants submit and vote on short statements; dimensionality reduction and clustering map the opinion space and surface statements with cross-cluster agreement. — [RECERCA](https://www.e-revistes.uji.es/index.php/recerca/article/view/5516)
- **Kialo.** Tree-structured debate: a thesis, then nested pro/con claims each answerable by further pro/con claims; educational research white paper by Chaudoin, Shapiro & Tingley (2017). — [Kialo Edu research](https://www.kialo-edu.com/research); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kialo)
- **Everything2 (1999–).** A "node" is a title; attached are multiple signed "writeups" by different users, so "diverse perspectives on the same topic ... coexist," ranked by votes and "C!" cools. — [Wikipedia: Everything2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Everything2)
- **Epistemic injustice in CSCW.** "Whose Knowledge is Valued? Epistemic Injustice in CSCW Applications," *PACM HCI* (CSCW) 2024: systematic exclusion of certain people and methods from the knowledge canon in peer-production platforms. — [ACM](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3687062)
- **Multiperspectivity (history education).** Stradling, *Multiperspectivity in History Teaching: A Guide for Teachers*, Council of Europe 2003 (16 translations): "a way of viewing, and a predisposition to view, historical events ... from different perspectives through drawing on procedures and processes which are fundamental to history as a discipline"; students need "ample opportunities to engage in voices, half-truths and partial points of view." — [CoE PDF](https://rm.coe.int/0900001680493c9e.pdf)
- **Transclusion / parallel documents (hypertext).** Nelson's Xanadu: "the fundamental problem of hypertext is the ability to see connections side by side"; the 1971 *Parallel Textface* shows two documents on one screen with visible links, later "transpointing windows"; transclusion keeps a quoted passage live-linked to its origin. — [mprove.de](https://mprove.de/visionreality/text/2.1.2_xanadu.html); [Xanalogical Structure](https://xanadu.com.au/ted/XUsurvey/xuDation.html)

### Inferences
- The recurring successful move is to make the unit of contribution a *claim with a stance* (typed link, pro/con, disputed-flag) rather than a paragraph, so that opposing content coexists by construction and the interface renders the structure. A prose wiki that wants labeled perspectives has to import that at the paragraph level: each paragraph carries a perspective label the way a Cohere node carries a link type.
- Reflect's "restate what you heard" and Wikipedia's "writing for the enemy" are the same primitive; a perspective section written by adherents could require an adjacent restatement by non-adherents as a quality gate.
- Everything2 is the only long-running site with several signed, whole-text treatments per title; its ranking by votes is exactly the "coverage vs. consensus" tension Wikidata's ranks avoid. Worth a look at what it lost (no shared factual spine).

### Gaps
- Searches for "multiperspective wiki", "pluralistic encyclopedia", "viewpoint wiki", "perspective-aware wiki", "polyphonic", "multivocal" + wiki returned no CSCW/CHI/Hypertext paper describing such a wiki; "multivocal"/"polyvocal" hits are all cultural-heritage/museum work (e.g., "Multivocal Exhibition," *JOCCH* 2024; "A Polyvocal Approach to Virtual Heritage," *JOCCH* 2024), not wikis.
- I could not verify any researcher named Sarah Morrison on this topic; omitted.
- DiSalvo's three tactics (revealing hegemony, reconfiguring the remainder, articulating collectives) are in the book but I could not open a source quoting them; not asserted above.
- "Design for Equivalence: Agonism for Collective ..." (ACM 2022, dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3536169.3537790) surfaced but was not opened.

## Q4. LLM-era (2022–2026): perspective-aware summarization, pluralistic alignment, article generation, bridging, and AI mediation — how do they keep perspectives distinct and labeled?

### Takeaway
Four distinct mechanisms recur: (1) *enumerate perspectives first, then generate per perspective* (STORM's simulated writers, PERSPECTRUM/PerSphere's claim→perspective→evidence schema, PUMA's perspective-tagged summaries, Plurals' persona agents); (2) *keep a latent viewpoint axis out of the score* (Community Notes' matrix factorization, generalized as bridging); (3) *proportional representation of a slate of statements* (generative social choice); (4) *iterated consensus drafting with a per-person preference model* (Habermas Machine). Only (1) preserves labels in the output; (2)–(4) select or synthesize across perspectives and, by the authors' own admission, can wash them out.

### Cited Findings
- **STORM (Shao, Jiang, Kanell, Xu, Khattab & Lam, NAACL 2024).** Pre-writing = "(1) discovering diverse perspectives in researching the given topic, (2) simulating conversations where writers carrying different perspectives pose questions to a topic expert grounded on trusted Internet sources, (3) curating the collected information to create an outline." Perspectives are mined from related Wikipedia articles (their tables of contents), plus a default "basic fact writer"; each perspective drives a multi-turn grounded QA; the perspectives are *not* preserved as labels in the final article — they shape coverage and outline. Results on FreshWiki: +25 pts organization, +10 pts coverage vs an outline-driven RAG baseline; editors found it useful for pre-writing; main failure is "red herrings" and source-bias transfer. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.14207); [project page](https://storm-project.stanford.edu/research/storm/)
- **Co-STORM (Jiang et al., "Into the Unknown Unknowns," EMNLP 2024).** Several LM "expert" agents with different roles discuss a topic, a moderator injects under-explored questions, the human observes or steers; discourse is tracked in a dynamic mind map and compiled into a report. 70% preferred it to search, 78% to a RAG chatbot. — [ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2024.emnlp-main.554/)
- **PERSPECTRUM (Chen, Khashabi, Yin, Callison-Burch & Roth, NAACL 2019).** Task of *substantiated perspective discovery*: given a claim, return a diverse set of stance-taking perspectives each backed by evidence paragraphs; 11,822 claims / 907 topics from debate sites. The data schema (claim → perspective(stance) → evidence) is the pre-LLM template for labeled multi-perspective output. — [ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/N19-1053/)
- **PerSphere (Dec 2024).** Benchmark of 1,064 instances, each with two opposing claims "supported by distinct, non-overlapping perspectives" across documents; end-to-end retrieval + multi-faceted summarization; LLMs struggle with long context and perspective extraction; a hierarchical multi-agent summarizer (HierSphere) helps most with many evidence documents. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.12588)
- **Perspective-aware healthcare answer summarization (Naik, Chandakacherla, Yadav & Akhtar, Findings of ACL 2024).** PUMA: 3,167 CQA threads with 6,193 perspective-tagged summaries; PLASMA: prompt/prefix-tuned controllable summarizer with an energy-controlled loss; +1.5–21% over baselines. Spawned the PerAnsSumm shared task (CL4Health @ NAACL 2025), where teams report LLMs "struggle with accurately capturing distinct perspectives" in long contexts. — [ACL](https://aclanthology.org/2024.findings-acl.942/); [PerAnsSumm](https://peranssumm.github.io/docs/); [UMB@PerAnsSumm](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.11118)
- **Unbiased perspective summarization by reranking (2025).** Generate several summaries, rerank by coverage and faithfulness rather than train. — [arXiv 2506.15925](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.15925)
- **Pluralistic alignment roadmap (Sorensen, Moore, Fisher, Gordon, Mireshghallah, Rytting, Ye, Jiang, Lu, Dziri, Althoff & Choi, ICML 2024).** Three operationalizations: *Overton* pluralism ("present a spectrum of reasonable responses"), *steerable* ("can steer to reflect certain perspectives"), *distributional* ("well-calibrated to a given population in distribution"); three benchmark types (multi-objective, trade-off-steerable, jury-pluralistic); evidence that "standard alignment procedures might reduce distributional pluralism." — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.05070); [PMLR](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v235/sorensen24a.html)
- **Value Kaleidoscope (Sorensen, Jiang, Hwang, Levine et al., AAAI 2024, oral).** Kaleido generates, explains and scores the relevance and valence of values/rights/duties in a situation, on the premise that "AI systems fit to averages by default, washing out potentially irreducible value conflicts"; humans preferred its value sets to GPT-4's for coverage. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.00779)
- **Modular Pluralism (Feng et al., EMNLP 2024).** Plug a pool of smaller *community LMs* into a base LLM; collaboration modes implement Overton, steerable and distributional pluralism; gains across six LLMs. — [ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2024.emnlp-main.240/)
- **Plurals (Ashkinaze et al., CHI 2025).** Agents (LLMs with ANES-derived, nationally representative personas) deliberate within customizable Structures under Moderators; motivated by "what if the solution is not to aim for a 'view from nowhere' but rather to leverage different viewpoints?"; simulated focus groups beat zero-shot output for the target audience in 75% of trials. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.17213); [ACM](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3706598.3713675)
- **Birdwatch / Community Notes (Wojcik, Hilgard, Judd, Mocanu, Ragain, Hunzaker, Coleman & Baxter, Twitter, arXiv 2210.15723, 2022).** Rating prediction r̂_un = μ + i_u + i_n + f_u·f_n with a *one-dimensional* factor per user and note; intercept regularization λ_i = 0.15 is 5× the factor regularization λ_f = 0.03, so "for a note to achieve a high intercept term, it must be rated helpful by raters with a diversity of factor vectors"; a note is "currently rated helpful" at i_n ≥ 0.40, "not helpful" below −0.08 (paper). Survey effects: notes chosen by the algorithm cut agreement with a misleading tweet by ~26% (Wave 3, N = 6,046), MF-selected notes by 41% in Wave 2 vs 22% for any note, "not sure" answers down 36%, no significant Democrat/Republican difference; 64% found notes helpful; a second factor lowered held-out RMSE only from .076 to .073. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.15723). Current open-source thresholds: CRH requires i_n ≥ 0.40 *and* |f_n| < 0.50; CRNH if i_n < −0.05 − 0.8·|f_n| or upper CI < −0.04. — [X ranking docs](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/twitter/communitynotes/main/documentation/under-the-hood/ranking-notes.md)
- **Bridging systems (Ovadya & Thorburn, Knight First Amendment Institute / arXiv 2301.09976, 2023).** Generalizes the above as *bridging-based ranking* — prefer content "receiving positive responses from users with diverse perspectives" — across recommenders, collective response systems and facilitated deliberation. — [Knight](https://knightcolumbia.org/content/bridging-systems)
- **LLM note writers.** Li et al., "Scaling Human Judgment in Community Notes with LLMs," *J. Online Trust & Safety* 2025 / arXiv 2506.24118: humans and LLMs both write notes; humans alone decide what shows; proposes RLCF (reinforcement learning from community feedback). — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.24118). Field evaluation (arXiv 2604.02592, 2026): one LLM writer published 1,614 notes on 1,597 tweets over Nov 2025–Jan 2026 vs 1,332 human notes on the same posts. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.02592)
- **Limits of one latent axis.** Andreou & Sirivianos, "Two Fault Lines: Latent Polarity Geometry in X Community Notes," arXiv 2609.21496, Sept 2026: refitting on 212.9M ratings finds a second, near-independent *institutional-trust* axis besides left/right; among low-polarization notes with 100+ ratings, publication falls from 71.5% to 11.7% as second-axis disagreement rises, because rank-1 "loses the second coordinate, which records that raters at one end of the second axis rate these notes helpful." — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.21496)
- **Habermas Machine (Tessler et al., *Science* 386, 18 Oct 2024).** Generates many candidate group statements (e.g., 32) from members' opinions; a personalized reward model predicts each member's ranking; rankings are aggregated by the Schulze method; members critique the winner and the loop repeats. Up to 2,110 UK participants in groups of ≤ 5; AI statements were endorsed over human mediators', produced in seconds, and left groups "less divided." Representation asymmetry: proportional to majority/minority after opinions, but "after processing the critiques, the HM tended to over-weight minority viewpoints." Stated risk: "short, bland statements that say little of substance" at scale. — [Knight analysis](https://knightcolumbia.org/content/can-ai-mediation-improve-democratic-deliberation); [Science](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.adq2852)
- **Generative social choice (Fish, Gölz, Parkes, Procaccia, Rusak, Shapira & Wüthrich, EC 2024).** Split the design into a social-choice process with provable representation guarantees given oracle queries, and LLM implementations of those queries; output is a "proportionally representative slate" of statements; in a 100-person U.S. abortion survey, 84/100 felt "excellently" or "exceptionally" represented by a five-statement slate. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.01291); [ACM EC](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3670865.3673547)

### Inferences
- For a wiki that wants labels, the transferable mechanism is STORM/PERSPECTRUM's *perspective-first* pipeline with the last step changed: emit per-perspective sections instead of merging into one outline. STORM already proves the perspective enumeration step is automatable from a topic's neighbours.
- Bridging's core trick — regularize the *shared* score so it can only be earned across viewpoints — is directly a rule for the shared factual spine of a multi-POV article: a sentence enters the common section only when raters from different labeled perspectives accept it; everything else stays in a perspective section. The 2026 "two fault lines" result warns that a single left/right label set is too coarse to make that gate honest.
- Generative social choice gives a principled way to decide *how many* perspective sections a topic gets (a slate with proportional-representation guarantees) rather than fixing labels a priori.
- Habermas-Machine-style synthesis is the opposite design goal (converge on one text); its own authors document that it over-weights minorities after critique and risks blandness — the two failure modes a labeled-perspective layout is meant to avoid.

### Gaps
- I did not find a 2022–2026 paper that generates a *Wikipedia-style article with perspectives retained as labeled sections*; STORM merges, PerSphere/PUMA summarize per perspective but not as encyclopedia articles.
- The Habermas Machine's exact endorsement percentages are in the *Science* paper (paywalled); I report the qualitative results from the Knight Institute analysis.
- The Community Alignment Dataset (arXiv 2507.09650, 2025) and LIVS/VITAL pluralistic benchmarks surfaced but were not opened.

## Q5. Historical / humanities design precedents for several labeled voices on one page

### Takeaway
The page-as-forum has a long typographic lineage: parallel columns for rival texts (Hexapla, polyglots, Griesbach's synopsis), a central text ringed by named commentaries that disagree with each other (glossed Bibles, the Talmud page), and encyclopedias that used signed articles and cross-references to stage contradiction (Diderot). Each fixes *who* speaks *where* on the page, which is precisely what a multi-POV article needs.

### Cited Findings
- **Origen's Hexapla (3rd c.).** Hebrew text, Hebrew in Greek letters, and the Aquila, Symmachus, Septuagint and Theodotion versions "in parallel vertical columns"; "probably had no exact precedent." — [Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/topic/biblical-literature/Origens-Hexapla)
- **Complutensian Polyglot (1514–17).** Modeled on the Hexapla; first printed Septuagint and Greek NT alongside Hebrew, Latin and Targum on one opening. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complutensian_Polyglot_Bible)
- **Griesbach, *Synopsis Evangeliorum* (1776).** Set Matthew, Mark and Luke "in parallel columns for ease of study. Instead of harmonizing them, he displayed their texts side by side, making both similarities and divergences apparent." — [Synoptic Gospels Primer](https://virtualreligion.net/primer/griesb.html)
- **Glossa ordinaria (12th c.).** "A central column of biblical text surrounded by marginal 'glosses' ... and interwoven with short interlinear glosses," commentary "typically dwarfing the actual text"; by the Carolingian period a two-column base-text/commentary page let readers "shift back and forth ... without having to retrieve other books." Salomon, *An Introduction to the Glossa Ordinaria as Medieval Hypertext* (2012). — [Notre Dame manuscript studies](https://sites.nd.edu/manuscript-studies/2015/09/17/reading-between-and-around-the-lines-an-introduction-to-glosses-and-commentaries/); [Salomon](https://www.barnesandnoble.com/w/an-introduction-to-the-glossa-ordinaria-as-medieval-hypertext-david-a-salomon/1110282640)
- **The Talmud page.** Soncino (1483) fixed Rashi on the inner margin and Tosafot on the outer; Bomberg's Venice edition (1519–23) fixed the pagination all later editions keep; before printing, commentaries lived in separate codices. — [NLI blog](https://blog.nli.org.il/en/daf_gemara/); [Jewish Action](https://jewishaction.com/cover-story/printing-the-shas/). Standard (Vilna) layout: Mishnah/Gemara centre, Rashi inner, Tosafot outer, with *Masoret HaShas* (parallel passages), *Ein Mishpat Ner Mitzvah* (halachic rulings) and *Torah Or* (verse references) as marginal cross-reference apparatus. — [Hadran](https://hadran.org.il/terms/the-layout-of-a-talmud-page/). The voices disagree by design: Tosafot (12th–13th c.) are printed "opposite Rashi's notes" and "consist mainly of strictures on Rashi's commentary"; the Tosafists "freely disputed his explanations." — [Wikipedia: Tosafot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tosafot)
- **Diderot & d'Alembert, *Encyclopédie* (1751–72).** 61,700 *renvois* (cross-references) across ~74,000 articles form "a lattice of inter-connections" that "intentionally and blatantly put contradictory articles into dialogue"; the cross-reference system also let readers "draw ironic and subversive comparisons between articles" past the censor. Contributors were identified by signature marks, though "around half of the articles ... lack a signature." — [DH abstract: renvois](https://dh-abstracts.library.virginia.edu/works/75); [Longreads](https://longreads.com/2019/01/30/how-diderots-encyclopedia-challenged-the-king/); [Encyclopedia of Diderot project](https://www.encyclopediaofdiderot.org/s/diderot/page/about-the-encyclopedia)
- **Xanadu (1971– ).** Parallel Textface / transpointing windows: two documents side by side with the links drawn between them as "a structural part of the writing." — [mprove.de](https://mprove.de/visionreality/text/2.1.2_xanadu.html)
- **Everything2 (1999– ).** Multiple signed write-ups under one node title, ranked by votes. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Everything2)

### Inferences
- Two layouts recur and map to two wiki designs: *columns* (perspectives are peers, aligned passage-by-passage — synopsis/polyglot; Manypedia/Omnipedia are the modern form) and *centre-plus-margins* (one shared text with named voices around it that may attack each other — Talmud/glossa; MultiBlog's margin-note rail is already this shape).
- Every precedent labels the voice typographically and *fixes its position* — Rashi is always inner, Tosafot always outer — so readers learn where a perspective lives before reading it. A multi-POV wiki should give each perspective a stable slot, not a reorderable list.
- Diderot's renvois are the precedent for *cross-perspective links as first-class content*: contradiction is staged by linking, not by merging.

### Gaps
- Signed-article practice in Britannica (11th ed.) and the Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, and *Mikraot Gedolot* (Bomberg's rabbinic Bible with multiple commentaries around the text) were not verified with sources in this pass; they are consistent with the pattern but not cited here.
- Bakhtin's "polyphony" is the obvious literary-theory anchor for "multivocal"; I did not source it and have not asserted it.
