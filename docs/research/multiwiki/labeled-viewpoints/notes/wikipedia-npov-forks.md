# Encyclopedia/wiki projects that rejected Wikipedia's single-NPOV article for multiple labeled points of view (2001–2026)

Research date: 2026-09-21. Scope: the *design mechanism* each project used (how a POV is named, whether POVs are separate pages or sections, navigation, authorship, linking) plus outcome. Wikinfo's own policy page and Infogalactic's roadmap were recovered from Wayback Machine captures because both live domains now serve unrelated content (see Gaps).

A quick taxonomy that emerges from the material, useful for the report writer:

| Mechanism | Who did it | Unit of POV | How labeled | Navigation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Main article sympathetic + *auxiliary* critical articles on the same wiki | Wikinfo (2003/2004–c.2017) | separate page | title suffix `Subject:A critical viewpoint` | reciprocal links "at the top" of both pages |
| Many whole encyclopedias, each one POV, aggregated & rated | Sanger / Encyclosphere (2019–) | separate *publisher* | `Publisher` field in ZWI metadata; declared "framework" (2025 thesis) | aggregator/reader app sorts by chosen ratings |
| One page, algorithmically filtered per reader's chosen perspective; Fact/Context/Opinion tiers | Infogalactic (promised 2016; never built) | version/tier of one page | reader preference, not author label | "preference filtering" (Phase 3–4, unbuilt) |
| One article, viewpoints attributed inline ("according to X") | Wikipedia NPOV (2001–) | sentence | in-text attribution | none needed; POV forks forbidden |
| Page-level bias disclosure box | Wikiversity Disclosures (proposed policy) | separate page | template naming the bias (e.g. left-wing, libertarian) | none prescribed |
| Structured argument tree, unsigned, both sides on one page | Wikiversity Wikidebate (c.2016–, discontinued) | argument | "Arguments for / against", objections nested | one page |
| One site = one POV | Conservapedia, RationalWiki (SPOV=snarky), Justapedia, Grokipedia | whole site | site identity | cross-site, none |

## Key question 1 — Wikinfo (Fred Bauder, 2003–) and the "sympathetic point of view" (SPOV)

### Takeaway
Wikinfo's mechanism was the simplest possible: the *main* article on a subject is written favorably (SPOV); dissenting views go into **separate auxiliary articles** titled with a colon suffix such as `[[United States:A critical viewpoint]]`, and the policy required that main and auxiliary articles be "prominently linked to at the top" of each other so a reader "can consider all the viewpoints". It ran on stock MediaWiki with no software support for the pattern beyond ordinary links; the site had ~34k pages in 2006 and ~45.7k when its policy page was written, drifted to a different host (wwtrade.org) by 2015–2017, and the wikinfo.org domain now serves unrelated content.

### Cited Findings
- Wikinfo "was launched in July 2003 by Fred Bauder, as a 'fork' of Wikipedia. Unlike Wikipedia, which uses 'neutral point of view', the main article on each subject in Wikinfo adopts a 'sympathetic point of view', points of view can be added, usually as articles linked to the main article." It had "more than 34000 pages in January 2006" and "there are no entries for the current and recent US presidents" at that time — [Talk:Wikinfo (enwiki), quoting a 2006 source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Talk:Wikinfo)
- Core rule (policy page `Wikinfo:Sympathetic point of view`, Wayback capture of the page as last modified 30 January 2010): "With the exception of extraordinarily offensive subjects, the main article on any subject should be written from a positive point of view, and in the case of biographies, from a point of view sympathetic to the subject. For example, the main article about the United Kingdom should focus on its accomplishments... Critical material can, and should be, covered in auxiliary articles which explore, for example, its role as an oppressive colonial power. **Auxiliary articles should be prominently linked to at the top of the main, positive, article on the subject, and vice-versa.**" — [Wikinfo:Sympathetic point of view (Wayback)](http://web.archive.org/web/2009id_/http://www.wikinfo.org/index.php/Wikinfo:Sympathetic_point_of_view)
- **Naming convention** for the alternative-POV page: "take an article on the United States. Definitely, to be written from a sympathetic point of view (SPOV). Additional articles will be necessary as one considers the reaction of the rest of the world and the critics of the United States. They are written under a title in the form: `[[United States:A critical viewpoint]]`. In that article one can feel free to fully express whatever negative light one might see the United States in. (This is kind of like a debate)" — [same](http://web.archive.org/web/2009id_/http://www.wikinfo.org/index.php/Wikinfo:Sympathetic_point_of_view)
- Plurality of critical pages, and the cross-linking rule: "for a country, a person, a movement the main article shows the matter in a good light. Additional articles may take various critical viewpoints. In the case of a concept the main article assumes the concept is useful and has meaning. The supplementary articles may show, in various way, how senseless, ambiguous and useless the concept is. **Link all such articles together at the top of each page, so that a reader of Wikinfo can consider all the viewpoints.**" — [same](http://web.archive.org/web/2009id_/http://www.wikinfo.org/index.php/Wikinfo:Sympathetic_point_of_view)
- Explicit rejection of NPOV: "Neutral point of view is an editorial policy of Wikipedia which is unattainable in many cases, leading to edit wars and arguments over whose view is more neutral than the others. This idealistic and unworkable policy is rejected here. No neutral point of view articles here on the Tiananmen Square Massacre with the viewpoint of the killers given equal weight to that of the victims." — [same](http://web.archive.org/web/2009id_/http://www.wikinfo.org/index.php/Wikinfo:Sympathetic_point_of_view)
- Conflict-resolution procedure and the section-vs-article choice: "in cases where the issue is point of view, what we do is add our own POV to the article, **either in a separate section or with a linked separate article**. We thus avoid silencing those voices rubbing us the wrong way... As a result, we don't have edit wars, and rarely have formal disagreements over content, since everyone has a clear outlet to express their point of view." Also: "we don't even have a page for deletions! Consider that we are now just as voluminous (45,690 articles) as Wikipedia was a couple of years ago" — [same](http://web.archive.org/web/2009id_/http://www.wikinfo.org/index.php/Wikinfo:Sympathetic_point_of_view)
- Threshold for splitting: "If a typical 'pro-and-con' treatment within an article satisfies all sides in a debate, then nothing more is required. But, if various perspectives cannot coexist in a single article, then each can have separate articles, each giving their various points of view." The page dates the approach: "The approach was initiated on Wikinfo in 2004 in reaction to NPOV" — [same](http://web.archive.org/web/2009id_/http://www.wikinfo.org/index.php/Wikinfo:Sympathetic_point_of_view)
- Limits (no sympathetic article for "extraordinarily offensive subjects, such as Hitler, National Socialism, rape or murder"), and the worked examples list ("Should the article on cancer be sympathetic to cancer? About how to avoid it and cure it.") — [same](http://web.archive.org/web/2009id_/http://www.wikinfo.org/index.php/Wikinfo:Sympathetic_point_of_view)
- Effect on imported Wikipedia content: "Articles newly imported or updated from Wikipedia will be written to a certain extent from NPOV... Some articles have been more or less left as is, some split into parts, some rewritten altogether." And the intended payoff: "Wikinfo's editorial policy results in a coherent article on both Israel and Palestine, and both Democrats and Republicans." — [same](http://web.archive.org/web/2009id_/http://www.wikinfo.org/index.php/Wikinfo:Sympathetic_point_of_view)
- Wikinfo's "About" page (as currently mirrored): "Generally the main article on a person or institution should present the subject in a positive light, (sympathetic point of view)"; "Critical or alternative viewpoints should also be presented, but not necessarily in the main article on the subject"; for concepts, "articles on the subject from alternate viewpoints are expected and encouraged"; SPOV described as "a way of encouraging a pluralism of content, rather than limiting content to an unattainable encyclopedic goal"; it also allowed "Original research" and "signed article[s]" — [Wikinfo about page](https://www.wikinfo.org/w/index-php/wikinfo_about.html); [Talk:Wikinfo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Talk:Wikinfo)
- Meta-Wiki's "Point of view" essay classifies Wikinfo as "the simplest dual-article model: sympathetic content in one article, critical analysis in another" — "a sympathetic point of view convention for the main article, but allows critical point of view to be separately stated in a parallel article" (page last edited 30 May 2021) — [Meta: Point of view](https://meta.wikimedia.org/wiki/Point_of_view)
- Later life of the site: a 2015 revision of the same policy page (last modified 27 October 2015, parser-cached 1 July 2017) was served from `wwtrade.org/English/index.php?title=Wikinfo:Sympathetic_point_of_view`, i.e. Wikinfo had moved to a subpath of another domain by then — [Wayback capture via wikinfo.org/English path](http://web.archive.org/web/2015id_/http://www.wikinfo.org/English/index.php/Wikinfo:Sympathetic_point_of_view)
- Wayback CDX for `wikinfo.org/` shows small (~1 KB) 200 responses through July 2020 and a different, larger page from January 2021; today `www.wikinfo.org` serves a JavaScript redirect stub and a `/w/index-php/...html` static "about" mirror — observed 2026-09-21 via curl (see Gaps for the missing closure date).

### Inferences
- Wikinfo is the only project in this survey that ran a *many-labeled-POVs-per-topic* scheme in production for a decade-plus. Its mechanism needed zero software: (a) a naming convention using MediaWiki's colon-in-title (which MediaWiki treats as an ordinary title, not a namespace, unless configured), (b) a manual reciprocal-link rule, (c) a norm against deletion so that adding a rival page is always the preferred move.
- The "sympathetic" main article is effectively a *privileged default POV* ("the subject's own"), with critical POVs as satellites; it is not symmetric multi-POV. The policy page itself admits many topics need no POV at all ("overtime").
- The design offloads *discovery* of rival pages entirely onto the manual top-of-page links — there is no index of "all viewpoints on X", which is the first thing a software implementation would add.

### Gaps
- I could not find a primary statement of when Wikinfo stopped operating or why (Wayback evidence only shows the move to wwtrade.org by 2015–2017 and unrelated content on wikinfo.org by 2021). Wikipedia has no current article on Wikinfo (it is not in the "List of content forks of Wikipedia").
- I found no count of how many `X:A critical viewpoint` pages actually existed, i.e. how often the mechanism was used versus simply importing Wikipedia articles.

## Key question 2 — Larry Sanger: competing articles, ratings, Knowledge Standards Foundation / Encyclosphere / ZWI

### Takeaway
Sanger's line runs from a 2019 call for a decentralized "encyclosphere" (many publishers, public ratings in a common feed, competing reader apps) to a September 2025 thesis that Wikipedia itself should "enable competing articles written within explicitly declared frameworks, each aiming at neutrality within its own framework" and "let the public rate articles". What is *built* (KSF, 2019–) is an aggregation layer: ZWI files carrying a `Publisher` field and a two-number `Rating`, ~3 million ZWI files from 66 encyclopedias by early 2024, read through EncycloReader/EncycloSearch. The "framework" label and reader-facing ratings remain proposals; the shipped unit of POV is the *publisher*, not a declared framework.

### Cited Findings
- 7 October 2019 proposal: "What if *all* of humanity were empowered to write encyclopedia articles, and rate them, as part of a completely decentralized knowledge network?"; a network "that decentralizes encyclopedias, exactly as the Blogosphere has done for blogs"; ratings "must be tied to relatively stable identities, and require one vote, one person", suggested via "public keys associated with various identity attributes" with third parties able to construct whitelists readers selectively trust; standards to be set by discussion on "a group blog" — [Sanger, "Want to help build an open encyclopedia network—an 'Encyclosphere'?" (2019-10-07)](https://larrysanger.org/2019/10/ksf/)
- KSF intro text (mechanism): "Raters—the general public, including people identified as experts—should be able to rate those articles"; "Users should be able to sort and re-sort articles according to all ratings, or selected ratings"; "The control over whose ratings to pay attention to should always be in the hands of the user"; "Many competing apps, all around the world, use the aggregated data to build encyclopedia readers according to their own editorial standards." — [KSF intro](https://www.ksf.sanger.io/intro). A secondary summary adds: "The data for both articles and ratings are published according to standards, or a single common format, in a feed, similar to an RSS feed"; "a decentralized encyclopedia network... that supports multiple, competing articles on all topics"; apps with "none being privileged or 'official'"; Dec 2022 projects: EncycloReader, Encyclosearch, FactSeek, Minifeed — [HandWiki: Encyclosphere](https://handwiki.org/wiki/Encyclosphere)
- "Wikipedia Is Badly Biased" (14 May 2020): argues the neutrality policy is defunct; defines a neutral article as one written "not to take sides on issues of controversy" so that readers cannot "detect the editors' political alignment"; remedy is external — "an independent and decentralized encyclopedia network... We will certainly develop such a network, but if it is to remain fully independent of all governmental and big corporate interests, funds are naturally scarce and it will take time." It does *not* propose multiple articles inside Wikipedia — [Sanger 2020-05-14](https://larrysanger.org/2020/05/wikipedia-is-badly-biased/)
- *Essays on Free Knowledge* (Sanger Press, first published 2020; paperback launch announced Sept/Oct 2020) collects "Wikipedia's Original Neutrality Policy", "Why Neutrality?", and "Introducing the Encyclosphere" (chapters 3, 4, 10) — [book PDF front matter](https://sanger.io/pdf/book.pdf); [larrysanger.org launch post, Oct 2020](https://larrysanger.org/2020/10/my-new-book-is-launched-in-paperback-here-are-some-quotes/)
- "Nine Theses on Wikipedia" (29 September 2025): Thesis 2 — "Enable competing articles written within explicitly declared frameworks, each aiming at neutrality within its own framework"; the rationale is that neutrality is impossible "when one perspective monopolizes editorial legitimacy", so frameworks are declared upfront "so readers understand the article's analytical lens" and each article "pursues neutrality within that stated framework rather than claiming false universality". Thesis 7 — "Let the public rate articles": "a system of public rating and feedback for Wikipedia articles is long overdue"; start by making objective data public and developing "an open source AI rating system", with human raters who are provably human and external to the editor community. Sanger proposes Wikipedia join the Encyclosphere ("collect all the encyclopedia articles in the world in a single decentralized network") or use it as the fallback if competing articles are not enabled — [Sanger, Nine Theses](https://larrysanger.org/nine-theses/); date and critique from [D. F. Lovett, Edit History substack](https://edithistory.substack.com/p/larry-sangers-nine-theses-on-wikipedia)
- The same substack review ranks competing articles and public ratings among Sanger's "three worst ideas", calling the competing-articles thesis "a mess" and the ratings thesis a recipe for "brigading and manipulation" — [Edit History](https://edithistory.substack.com/p/larry-sangers-nine-theses-on-wikipedia)
- ZWI ("Zipped Wiki") format: a ZIP with required `article.wikitext`, `article.html`, `metadata.json`, `media.json`; optional `article.xhtml`, `article.tex`, `article.dokuwiki`, `signature.json` ("a token signed with a private key"), `plugins.json`, `data/media/[namespace]/`, `data/attic/[namespace]/` (old revisions). `metadata.json` fields include `ZWIversion`, `Title`, `Topics`, `Categories`, `Publisher`, `Rating` ("Two-number array: [score, hits]"), `SourceURL`, `License`, `LastModified`, `PublicationDate`. "Different publishers" and the `Publisher` field are what distinguish two articles on the same title — [Encyclosphere wiki: ZWI file format](https://wiki.encyclosphere.org/ZWI_file_format)
- The `Rating` field "consists of 2 numbers: total score (defined by a publisher) and the number of hits" — i.e. today's rating is publisher-supplied, not reader-supplied — [search summary of the ZWI page](https://wiki.encyclosphere.org/ZWI_file_format)
- EncycloReader: original version launched 1 December 2021; software transferred to KSF October 2021; reads articles "in a unified representation, independent of the formats of external encyclopedias"; initial sources Wikipedia, Citizendium, HandWiki, Scholarpedia, Ballotpedia, EduTechWiki, Wikitia, Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy; the wiki page does not describe a side-by-side or per-topic grouping UI — [Encyclosphere wiki: Encycloreader](https://wiki.encyclosphere.org/Encycloreader)
- Scale: "close to 3 million ZWI files from 66 encyclopedias" (KSF Q4 2023 newsletter, published 16 February 2024); EncycloSearch filters by category; the newsletter contains no statement on how duplicate topics across encyclopedias are presented — [KSF Q4 2023 newsletter](https://blog.encyclosphere.org/q4-2023-newsletter)
- Sanger's Everipedia stint (CIO December 2017 – resigned 18 October 2019): Everipedia "did not allow censorship on any topic for sourced articles", planned token-staked edits, and became read-only in October 2022 (successor IQ.wiki); the Wikipedia article does not say it allowed competing articles per topic — [Everipedia (enwiki)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Everipedia)

### Inferences
- Sanger's *shipped* answer to "how is a POV labeled?" is provenance: the encyclopedia that published the article (ZWI `Publisher`). A reader who wants a conservative or Catholic or skeptic article on X picks the publisher, not a framework tag. Framework labels, reader ratings with identity, and rating feeds are 2019/2025 designs still unimplemented as of the sources found.
- His 2025 within-Wikipedia proposal is the closest modern restatement of Wikinfo's model, with the improvement that every competing article is labeled by *declared framework* rather than defaulting one to "sympathetic".
- The ratings side has moved from "one vote, one person via public keys" (2019) to "open source AI rating" plus provably-human external raters (2025) — a signal that identity-bound public rating was the part that did not get built.

### Gaps
- Could not retrieve EncycloReader's help page (connection refused) or a live EncycloSearch results page, so I cannot describe how it renders several publishers' articles for one title (tabs? list? ranking rule?). The Encyclosphere wiki page for EncycloReader says nothing about it.
- The full text of "Introducing the Encyclosphere" (book chapter 10, 2020) was not retrievable; only the PDF's front matter came through. The mechanism quotes above come from the KSF intro page and the 2019 post.
- No 2021–2024 Sanger essay proposing competing articles *inside* Wikipedia was found before the September 2025 Nine Theses.

## Key question 3 — Infogalactic (2016–): "context and relativity levels" / perspective filters

### Takeaway
Infogalactic (Vox Day, launched October 2016 as a fork of English Wikipedia) specified, in its "Seven Canons"/introduction and a five-phase roadmap, that each page would carry "two levels of Context and Opinion", that content would be "tri-level" (Fact, Context, Opinion), and that readers would "select their preferred perspective and automatically see the version of the subject page that is closest to it" via "Relativity, Reliability, and Notability" algorithms and "Preference filtering". None of that was built: by the roadmap's own 6 May 2017 revision only speed, ads, editor tiers and "dynamic page updates" were marked complete, a hostile but specific 2020 audit found the site still on MediaWiki 1.27.1 with "zero technical progress" since May 2017, and as of 2026-09-21 infogalactic.com serves a landing page for an unrelated "Infogalactic AG — Agentic Operations".

### Cited Findings
- Launch press release, 10 October 2016: users can "select their preferred perspective and automatically see the version of the subject page that is closest to it" based on algorithms using "Relativity, Reliability, and Notability"; worked example: "A supporter of Hillary Clinton will see a different version of the current Donald Trump page than a Donald Trump supporter will"; the aim is to make "the function of thought police irrelevant through technology"; the Seven Canons include "Infogalactic does not define reality", "Infogalactic is written from an objective point of view", "Facts are facts"; Phase Two was to add "Context and Opinion sub-pages", "dynamic page updates", and "an alpha version of perspective filtering" — [Infogalaxians blog, "An online encyclopedia without bias or thought police" (2016-10-10)](https://infogalactic.blogspot.com/2016/10/an-online-encyclopedia-without-bias-or.html)
- Introduction page (Wayback): pages "are categorized in a variety of ways, including Relativity, Notability, and Reliability to allow the user to prioritize his personalized perspective"; "The addition of perspective filters and two levels of Context and Opinion to every page means that the average editor's contribution is much less likely to be deleted for political reasons or fall victim to edit wars over controversial pages"; "Infogalactic is designed around the idea that the user should be permitted to decide what information is relevant to him, not 500 ideologically-driven thought police"; "This is Phase One Infogalactic, which is little more than a functioning fork of Wikipedia that serves as a proof-of-concept" — [Infogalactic:Introduction (Wayback 2017)](https://web.archive.org/web/2017/https://infogalactic.com/info/Infogalactic:Introduction)
- Roadmap 2.0 (19 October 2016): replace MediaWiki with a custom engine "codename DONTPANIC" ("We estimate 9 months until the inital beta implementation"); Phase Two "Relativity, Reliability, and Notability 1.0 algorithms"; Phase Three "Tri-level page content: Fact, Context, Opinion", "Verified autobiography sub-pages", "Preference filtering"; three editor tiers: "create page only, create and add content only, and create, add, and remove content" — [Infogalaxians blog, "Roadmap 2.0" (2016-10-19)](https://infogalactic.blogspot.com/2016/10/roadmap-20.html)
- Roadmap page as last modified 6 May 2017 (Wayback, cached 2019-07-16): "Infogalactic is presently operational in Phase Two." Phase One items all COMPLETE (image load, search speed, Brave integration, ad server, "Additional administration and editing levels"). Phase Two "IN PROGRESS": "DONTPANIC engine" (no mark), "Sub-sites wikimedia - COMPLETE", "Dynamic page updates - COMPLETE", "Improved Database categories" and "Relativity, Reliability, and Notability 1.0 algorithms" (no mark). Phase Three: "Tri-level page content: Fact, Context, Opinion; Verified autobiography sub-pages; Preference filtering; Initial gamification...; Safe Mode; Gab integration; User Interface 2.0 Beta". Phase Four: "Preference filtering launch; User Interface 2.0 launch; Personal pages Alpha". Phase Five: "External API; Personal pages Beta; Communication system Alpha" — [Infogalactic:Roadmap (Wayback 2018–2019)](https://web.archive.org/web/2018/https://infogalactic.com/info/Infogalactic:Roadmap)
- Hostile audit, 15 September 2020: "A check of the source code of newly created articles at Voxopedia shows that it still uses MediaWiki 1.27.1. The current version that Wikipedia uses is 1.34.2"; perspective filters, tri-level content and DONTPANIC "were never implemented"; "the whole operation has made zero technical progress since [May 2017]"; editors "import several Wikipedia pages into Voxopedia in a few chunks at a time" — [Camestros Felapton, "Return to Voxopedia"](https://camestrosfelapton.wordpress.com/2020/09/15/return-to-voxopedia/) (critic; the MediaWiki-version check is verifiable in principle)
- Status 2026-09-21: `https://infogalactic.com/` and `https://infogalactic.com/info/Infogalactic:Roadmap` both return a landing page for "Infogalactic AG — Agentic Operations... ZUG · CET... Architecting AI-powered solutions for the modern enterprise" with no wiki content — observed by curl/WebFetch during this research (no third-party source found for the transition).

### Inferences
- What was specified was *not* multiple labeled articles: it was one page per topic with (a) three content *tiers* (Fact / Context / Opinion) presumably as sub-pages, and (b) reader-side *filtering* choosing among page versions by a "Relativity" score against the reader's declared perspective. The POV label lives on the reader's profile, not on the text. That is closer to personalization/recommendation than to Wikinfo's or Sanger's explicit, author-declared frameworks.
- Nothing in the published material defines what "Relativity" measures or how a "version closest to" a perspective would be selected among edits; it was a promise without an algorithm. The only concrete deliverable in the direction of "layers" was editor tiers.

### Gaps
- No definition of the Relativity/Reliability/Notability algorithms was ever published that I could find; the roadmap only names them.
- I could not confirm from a neutral source when the Infogalactic wiki went offline or when the domain changed hands; the only evidence is the current landing page.

## Key question 4 — Wikipedia's own handling: POV forks, perennial proposals, attribution, "Criticism of", Wikiversity

### Takeaway
Wikipedia keeps exactly one article per topic and moves plurality *inside the sentence*: opinions are attributed in-text ("praised by baseball insiders such as Al Kaline and Joe Torre"), weighted by prominence in sources, and integrated rather than segregated into pro/con sections; a second article on the same subject "to evade the neutrality policy" is a POV fork and is deleted or merged, with "Criticism of X" pages tolerated only when sources treat the criticism itself as a topic. The Perennial-proposals page, checked in raw wikitext on 2026-09-21, has **no entry** about multiple versions of an article — the idea has not even reached "perennially rejected" status; the closest thing is a 2006 orphaned essay, "Wikipedia:Multiple points of view". Wikiversity is the Wikimedia project that formally departs from NPOV, via a proposed "Disclosures" policy (a bias box at the head of each learning resource) and the Wikidebate format (unsigned arguments for/against on one page), which is now marked discontinued.

### Cited Findings
- NPOV's attribution device: "biased statements of opinion can be presented only with in-text attribution"; example: "John Doe's baseball skills have been praised by baseball insiders such as Al Kaline and Joe Torre"; weight: "all significant viewpoints that have been published by reliable sources, in proportion to the prominence of each viewpoint"; "Views held by a tiny minority should not be represented except in articles devoted to those views" — [WP:NPOV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Neutral_point_of_view)
- NPOV on structure and forks: "A POV fork is an attempt to evade the neutrality policy by creating a new article about a subject that is already treated"; all significant viewpoints belong in one article except spinoffs; segregating content "based solely on the apparent POV of the content itself" is discouraged — debates should be integrated into the narrative rather than isolated into pro/con sections; "The name chosen for a topic can give an appearance of bias" — [WP:NPOV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Neutral_point_of_view)
- Content forking guideline: a POV fork is content created to bypass NPOV "often to promote or suppress particular viewpoints"; prohibited: "Creating multiple articles of the same type covering identical subjects" and "Splitting articles to advocate particular stances rather than resolve disagreements through consensus"; acceptable: spinout articles (e.g. "Death of Michael Jackson"), summary style, and "Criticism of X" articles that "must include suitably-weighted positive and negative opinions, and/or rebuttals" with a preference for titles like "reception" or "perception" — [WP:Content forking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Content_forking)
- "Criticism" essay: "Dedicated 'Criticism of ...' articles are sometimes appropriate for organizations, businesses, philosophies, religions, or political outlooks, provided the sources justify it"; default is that "Negative criticism should be interwoven throughout the topical or thematic sections"; "Creating separate articles with the sole purpose of grouping the criticisms... is generally considered a POV fork"; Jimmy Wales quoted that criticism sections are "a troll magnet"; the page is an essay, "not a Wikipedia policy" — [WP:Criticism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Criticism)
- Perennial proposals: grepping the page's raw wikitext (2026-09-21) for "point of view", "POV", "multiple versions" and "neutral" finds NPOV mentioned only under "Advertising" ("Wikipedia is supposed to be neutral, which advertising by definition is not"; see-also "Spanish Wikipedia and Enciclopedia Libre Universal en Español") and "Only allow the truth in articles"; there is no entry for allowing multiple versions or POV-labeled articles — [WP:Perennial proposals (raw)](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Wikipedia:Perennial_proposals&action=raw)
- "Wikipedia:Multiple points of view" (essay created by Reswik in 2006; orphaned): theorizes an evolution from NPOV to "MPOV"; NPOV assumes "one root balanced point of view is possible"; MPOV assumes "a set of points of view is always constructed from one point of view and thus cannot be avoided, hence there is always a set of points of view that may expand and not contract over time"; it proposes considering ways "such that points of view could be mixed in lots of different ways", judged "more relevant to... the social sciences and humanities than to the natural sciences"; no mechanism specified — [WP:Multiple points of view](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Multiple_points_of_view)
- Meta-Wiki "Point of view" essay lists the historical alternatives: "Multiple point of view... allowing for a number of different complete views on a topic to be shared"; the IPA/TIPAESA format used by openpolitics.ca and dkosopedia.com (position statements + arguments; featured issue "whether Canada should recognize Hamas"); an "issue/yes/no convention" ("yes, this is an issue" / "no" then named positions with arguments for, against, and counter-arguments); and Wikinfo's dual-article model; observes that views a discourse cannot accommodate "will still show up on talk pages, deletion discussions, and forked wikis" — [Meta: Point of view](https://meta.wikimedia.org/wiki/Point_of_view)
- Wikiversity Disclosures (proposed policy, last edited 22 March 2026): "when academic freedom leads Wikiversity participants beyond the restrictions of the traditional Wikimedia Foundation Neutral point of view (NPOV) policy, the Wikiversity pages that are exempted from NPOV must be marked as such"; each resource carries a disclosure box (scales icon) at the head of the page; "Developers of a resource need not disclose all their biases, merely the ones that they present in the coursework"; multi-perspective pages must "state all encompassed biases in their Disclosure"; enumerated POV labels include "NPOV, left-wing, right-wing, Christian conservative, libertarian, atheist, socialist" and educational-philosophy labels — [Wikiversity:Disclosures](https://en.wikiversity.org/wiki/Wikiversity:Disclosures)
- Wikiversity, unlike Wikipedia/Wiktionary/Wikibooks/Wikiquote, "does not have a strict neutral point of view policy", allowing scholars "to intensively explore different points of view... as long as biases are disclosed and high standards of scholarship are adhered to" — [search summary citing Wikiversity policy pages](https://en.wikiversity.org/wiki/Wikiversity:Policies)
- Wikidebate: "a collaborative learning project to gather and organize all arguments on controversial issues"; the project page is now marked inactive/archived: it "has been discontinued due to a lack of moderation and policy implementation" — [Wikiversity: Wikidebate](https://en.wikiversity.org/wiki/Wikidebate)
- Wikidebate guidelines: "Don't sign your arguments" ("The original author can always be traced back from the history of the debate"); "If two arguments are essentially the same, merge them together into one, keeping the best of each"; "If one argument is essentially two, split them apart"; "Wikidebates are organized compilations of arguments surrounding an issue. Therefore, try to add and improve arguments on both sides of the issue"; goal: "establish truth when the issue at hand is a matter of fact, or facilitate compromise when there are conflicting interests" — [Wikidebate/Guidelines](https://en.wikiversity.org/wiki/Wikidebate/Guidelines)
- Wikidebate structure per its maintainer (Sophivorus/Felipe Schenone, 31 Oct 2016 thread): "arguments are not signed and can therefore be improved upon by others"; arguments are coloured SUSTAINED or REFUTED, computed by a recursive "dialectic algorithm" (later "debate algorithm") from the objection tree — [LessWrong linkpost and comments](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/JHk54Kq4zkzHLiY2j/wikidebate); [Wikiversity: Debate algorithm](https://en.wikiversity.org/wiki/Dialectic_algorithm)

### Inferences
- Wikipedia's answer to the software builder is: label at the *claim* level (attribution string + source), never at the page level; make the page's neutrality a property of weighting, not of separate voices. The three tolerated exceptions — spinout articles, "Criticism of" when sourced as a topic, and "articles devoted to" a fringe view — are all still framed as single-NPOV articles about a narrower subject, never as rival treatments of the same subject.
- The absence of a perennial-proposals entry is itself evidence: multiple-version articles are not a recurring community demand inside Wikipedia; the demand shows up outside, as forks.
- Wikiversity's Disclosures box is the cleanest in-Wikimedia precedent for a *page-level POV label* with a controlled vocabulary, but it prescribes no linkage between differently-disclosed pages on the same topic.
- Wikidebate replaced "POV" with "argument" as the unit and used anonymity to decouple arguments from their holders — the opposite of a labeled-POV design — and it died of moderation cost, not of design.

### Gaps
- No talk-page archive discussion where "allow multiple POV articles" was formally proposed and rejected was located; the report writer should not claim it is a listed perennial proposal.
- Wikidebate's start date and number of debates were not found in the sources retrieved.

## Key question 5 — Forks driven by POV disagreement (one POV per site vs. many per article)

### Takeaway
Every POV-motivated fork after Wikinfo chose "one POV per site": Enciclopedia Libre (2002) loosened NPOV toward personal interpretation but kept one article per topic; Citizendium (2006–) kept a single "unbiased" article with expert approval; Conservapedia (2006–), RationalWiki ("snarky/scientific point of view"), Justapedia (2023) and Grokipedia (2025, LLM-written, no direct human editing) each publish one article per topic in the site's own voice. The "many POVs per article" pattern exists only in Wikinfo (in practice), Sanger's 2025 proposal (on paper) and Infogalactic's roadmap (never built).

### Cited Findings
- Enciclopedia Libre Universal en Español: forked 26 February 2002 by Edgar Enyedy over "concerns about censorship and the possibility of advertising on Wikipedia" (plus outdated software, denied server access, .com domain); it "allowed for a wider range of writing styles and content types. For instance, writers could reflect on philosophical questions, express personal interpretations of cultural issues, or present contrasting viewpoints on controversial subjects"; Enyedy later said it "was not intended to last. It was merely a form of pressure"; "By late 2024 the site had ceased operations altogether" — [Enciclopedia Libre (enwiki)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enciclopedia_Libre_Universal_en_Español); enwiki's fork list calls it "a 2002 fork of the Spanish Wikipedia created in opposition to perceived plans to add advertising" — [List of content forks of Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_content_forks_of_Wikipedia)
- Citizendium (launched by Sanger, 2006/March 2007): "its 'Neutrality Policy' favors the term unbiased" (Reagle, citing CZ:Neutrality Policy as of 25 November 2007); it experimented with "unforking" borrowed Wikipedia content — "rewriting it from scratch" — and in December 2007 chose a CC license so "Wikipedia and the Citizendium will be able to exchange content easily" — [Reagle, *Good Faith Collaboration* ch. 3](https://reagle.org/joseph/2010/gfc/chapter-3.html), [ch. 4](https://reagle.org/joseph/2010/gfc/chapter-4.html); enwiki lists it "Defunct (unforked in 2007)" — [List of content forks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_content_forks_of_Wikipedia)
- Conservapedia: in 2008 its front page recommended its "article of the year" so readers might "Discover what Wikipedia, the public school systems, and the liberal media don't want you to know about atheism"; its "Examples of Bias in Wikipedia" article listed 160 instances (June 2009); Reagle: "Conservapedia is but one of the first of many ideological user-generated encyclopedias likely to be started — though many soon fall into disuse", citing The Register's 2007 "Conservapedia Too Pinko? Try Metapedia" — [Reagle ch. 7](https://reagle.org/joseph/2010/gfc/chapter-7.html)
- RationalWiki "is written from a self-described 'snarky point of view' and 'scientific point of view' (both abbreviated as SPOV) rather than a 'neutral point of view' (NPOV), and publishes opinion, speculation, and original research"; snark "must support the site missions, not obstruct them" — [search summary citing RationalWiki:What is a RationalWiki article? and enwiki "Wikipedia is not RationalWiki"](https://rationalwiki.org/wiki/RationalWiki:What_is_a_RationalWiki_article) (RationalWiki itself returned 503 during this research; the acronym collision with Wikinfo's SPOV is coincidental)
- Justapedia "officially launched on August 9th, 2023", founded by Betty Wills (Wikipedia's "Atsme" since 2011) out of "a concern that Wikipedia was drifting steadily further away from its original principles of neutrality"; name = "Just an encyclopedia"; "Most of Justapedia's articles were created as mirrors of articles on Wikipedia, which Justapedia's users have begun modifying to reflect the newer site's policies and priorities" — [Quillette, "Introducing Justapedia" (2023-12-11)](https://quillette.com/2023/12/11/introducing-justapedia/)
- Grokipedia launched 27 October 2025 with "over 800,000 articles", "5.6 million articles by early 2026"; "Some entries are generated by Grok... while others were forked from Wikipedia"; "Users cannot directly edit Grokipedia articles, but logged-in users can suggest edits and report errors, with such submissions being reviewed and implemented by the Grok AI"; versions v0.1 (27 Oct 2025) and v0.2 (21 Nov 2025); "Around 25 April 2026, Grokipedia stopped reviewing suggested changes... with implementation of user requests having stopped a month earlier around 22 March" — [Grokipedia (enwiki)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grokipedia)
- A 2026 LLM-judge audit found "Grokipedia... rated less neutral than Wikipedia by all four AI judges, with bias favouring economically right-wing politicians" — [arXiv 2607.15146](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.15146); a commentary contrasts Wikipedia, where "disputes, edits, and talk pages make disagreement transparent and correctable", with Grokipedia, where "authorship is singular, automated, and invisible, bias becomes latent" — [TechPolicy.Press](https://www.techpolicy.press/with-grokipedia-topdown-control-of-knowledge-is-new-again/)
- Everipedia (2015 fork; Sanger CIO Dec 2017–Oct 2019) "did not allow censorship on any topic for sourced articles" and had looser notability; read-only since October 2022 — [Everipedia (enwiki)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Everipedia)

### Inferences
- The fork lineage shows the *label* migrating from the page title (Wikinfo) to the domain name (everyone after). A site-level POV is cheaper to run (no cross-linking, no rival-page discovery) but gives the reader no way to compare, which is precisely the gap Sanger's aggregator tries to fill from the outside.
- Longevity correlates with having a community identity, not with the POV mechanism: Conservapedia and RationalWiki (single-POV, opinionated) have outlived Wikinfo, Citizendium's ambitions, Infogalactic and (apparently) Grokipedia's editing loop.

### Gaps
- Conservapedia's own "Commandments" page and Justapedia's own "About" page could not be fetched (connection refused / 403); their policies are cited via Reagle and Quillette respectively.
- No source was found confirming whether Justapedia has any mechanism beyond single-article neutrality (e.g. per-article POV labels); absent evidence, treat it as one-POV-per-site.

## Key question 6 — Tkacz (2015) on forking as dissent; Reagle (2010) on NPOV as collaborative norm

### Takeaway
Reagle treats NPOV as simultaneously "the epistemic foundation for the project" and "the intentional stance contributors should take while interacting", warns specifically against "POV forking in which two articles with opposing points of view arise in place of a single NPOV article", and predicts that ideological encyclopedias will keep being founded and "soon fall into disuse". Tkacz argues that forking is the *sanctioned* form of dissent in open projects — revered as "a defense against tyranny and guarantor of democracy" — but that this reverence lets a project treat every internal policy intervention as illegitimate "closure", so disagreement is exported rather than accommodated. Neither book endorses multi-POV wikis; Reagle's evidence (Conservapedia, Metapedia) and Tkacz's (the Spanish fork, which its own leader called "merely a form of pressure") both point toward exit as the realistic channel.

### Cited Findings
- Reagle: "the notion of NPOV not only provides the epistemic foundation for the project, but also the intentional stance contributors should take while interacting"; articles "are considered to be without bias when they 'describe the debate fairly rather than advocating any side'"; contributors "should be careful in articles about controversy to avoid... POV forking in which two articles with opposing points of view arise in place of a single NPOV article" — [Reagle, *Good Faith Collaboration* (MIT Press 2010), ch. 3](https://reagle.org/joseph/2010/gfc/chapter-3.html)
- Reagle on forks as the release valve: the Spanish fork (2002, over advertising) has since been "resolved and Spanish Wikipedia has superseded the fork"; Sanger's "dissatisfaction with the lack of respect for expert contributors at Wikipedia led him to start the Citizendium project"; Citizendium contributors feared that under the GFDL "Wikipedia could import the improved Citizendium content back into itself. This was unacceptable to those who wished to distinguish themselves" — [Reagle ch. 4](https://reagle.org/joseph/2010/gfc/chapter-4.html)
- Reagle on ideological forks: "Conservapedia is but one of the first of many ideological user-generated encyclopedias likely to be started — though many soon fall into disuse" — [Reagle ch. 7](https://reagle.org/joseph/2010/gfc/chapter-7.html)
- Tkacz, *Wikipedia and the Politics of Openness* (University of Chicago Press, 2015): examines "how the key concepts of openness—including collaboration, ad-hocracy, and the splitting of contested projects through 'forking'—play out in reality", through "edit wars, article deletion policies, user access levels" — [UCP catalogue](https://press.uchicago.edu/ucp/books/book/chicago/W/bo19085555.html)
- Tkacz on how forking is revered: "(the threat of) forking is [seen as] a defense against tyranny and guarantor of democracy, it produces a form of consensus, transfers power from leaders to followers, achieves practical meritocracies, de-monopolizes power, ensures maximum freedom, and brings about diversity and radical innovation"; his critique: "you can't really address some of the glaringly obvious issues with policy-like interventions because doing so will result in 'closure'" — [Times Higher Education review, 1 Jan 2015](https://www.timeshighereducation.com/books/wikipedia-and-the-politics-of-openness-by-nathaniel-tkacz/2017640.article); openness "is countered by a 'closure' where community debates determined what is excluded" — [Goodreads/publisher summaries](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/20948486-wikipedia-and-the-politics-of-openness)
- Tkacz's forking argument first appeared as "The politics of forking paths" (pp. 94–109) in *Critical Point of View: A Wikipedia Reader* (Lovink & Tkacz eds., Institute of Network Cultures, 2011), alongside "'Good luck with your wikiPAIDia': reflections on the 2002 fork of the Spanish Wikipedia", an interview with Edgar Enyedy (pp. 110–118) — [Goldsmiths Research Online](https://research.gold.ac.uk/id/eprint/34115/); [WRAP](http://wrap.warwick.ac.uk/53293/)
- Enyedy on the Spanish fork: it "was not intended to last. It was merely a form of pressure" — [Enciclopedia Libre (enwiki)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enciclopedia_Libre_Universal_en_Español)

### Inferences
- Both authors imply the same structural point for a software designer: a single-NPOV article is a *social* device (it forces co-editing of one text), and once you allow rival texts you lose the forcing function; the community then either exits (fork) or fragments into rival pages with no pressure to read each other — which is Wikinfo's outcome (satellite pages) and the Encyclosphere's (rival publishers) in different clothing.
- Tkacz's "closure" observation cuts against multi-POV wikis from the other side: a project that refuses internal policy interventions to keep itself "open" will not be able to enforce the labeling and linking discipline that a multi-POV scheme needs (Wikinfo's reciprocal-link rule, Wikiversity's disclosure box, Sanger's declared frameworks all require exactly such enforcement).

### Gaps
- I could not access the text of Tkacz's forking chapter itself (book or 2011 essay); quotations are from a review and publisher copy. Whether Tkacz addresses Wikinfo or multi-POV projects by name is unconfirmed.
- Reagle's chapters fetched contain no mention of Wikinfo or "sympathetic point of view" (grep of all eight online chapters found none), so the book should not be cited for a verdict on Wikinfo specifically.
