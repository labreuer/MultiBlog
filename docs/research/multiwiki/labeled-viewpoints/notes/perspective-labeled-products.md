# Products and reference works that present one topic through explicitly labeled perspectives

Scope: how each product labels its perspectives, who assigns the labels, whether the perspectives are separate texts or one text with per-perspective sections, and how the side-by-side comparison is done in the UI. Compiled 2026-09-21. Where a product's own page could not be fetched (403), the fallback source is named and the item is marked.

## Key question 1: News/issue products — label taxonomies and how the reader is shown the comparison

### Takeaway
Every news product in this class labels the *source outlet*, not the argument, on a single left–right axis (5 or 7 buckets), assigned by a third party (AllSides, Ad Fontes, MBFC) or by the product's own editors; the reader is shown either three parallel columns of *separate* articles (AllSides, Ground News), a curated digest with a section per side in *one* text (Flip Side, Tangle), or a fact block plus AI-drafted, human-edited "narratives" labeled per side (Verity). Only ProCon labels the *argument* itself (Pro/Con), and it is the only one whose two sides answer the same question.

### Cited Findings

**AllSides (2012– )**
- Founded 2012 by John Gable (ex-Republican aide, Netscape) and Scott McDonald. Five-point scale: Left, Lean Left, Center, Lean Right, Right; AllSides itself says the five buckets "sacrifice precision in favor of simplicity" versus gradient charts. Per Wikipedia's summary of the method: crowd-sourced reviews by volunteers overseen by two staff of differing politics, plus staff editorial review, plus community feedback that can trigger re-rating. — [Wikipedia: AllSides](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AllSides) (AllSides' own methodology page returned HTTP 403 to the fetcher; the "Editorial Review / Blind Bias Survey / Independent Review / Third Party Analysis / Community Feedback" method names could not be confirmed from the primary page in this session.)
- Poynter (2021): AllSides' Julie Mastrine calls the blind bias survey (readers rate articles without seeing the source) "one of (AllSides') most robust bias rating methodologies," and concedes the five buckets' limit: "we have to put somebody in a category when, in reality, it's kind of a spectrum." — [Poynter, "Should you trust media bias charts?"](https://www.poynter.org/fact-checking/media-literacy/2021/should-you-trust-media-bias-charts/)
- AllSides also trains raters on "16 types of bias including spin, slant, sensationalism, bias by omission, and analysis presented as fact" (search-snippet claim, attributed to a Factually.co explainer; not verified against AllSides' own page). — [Factually.co](https://factually.co/fact-checks/media/allsides-ground-news-media-bias-methods-reliability-b342d2)
- Balanced Search: "stories from the left, center, and right appear side-by-side"; the pitch is that "there is no such thing as unbiased news," and the tool claims "800+ Media Bias Ratings." Presentation is three columns of *separate* articles by different outlets, each column headed by the bias label. — [AllSides Balanced Search](https://www.allsides.com/search); [App Store listing](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/allsides-balanced-news/id1535521873)
- Critique: a 2025 Columbia Journalism Review piece questioned AllSides' credibility partnerships and whether bias-monitoring companies themselves warrant evaluation. — [Wikipedia: AllSides](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AllSides)

**Ground News (2018– , Kitchener, Ont.)**
- Seven bias buckets: Far Left, Left, Lean Left, Center, Lean Right, Right, Far Right. Bias for an outlet is the *average* of three external raters — AllSides, Ad Fontes Media, Media Bias/Fact Check — and "when outlets lack complete coverage" Ground News takes "an average of the ratings available." Factuality is a separate 5-tier label (Very Low / Low / Mixed / High / Very High). Ownership is an 8-way taxonomy Ground News built itself (Media Conglomerates, Private Equity, Individual, Government, Telecom, Corporation, Independent, Other) from "2,276 news outlets and counting." — [Ground News Rating System](https://ground.news/rating-system)
- Blindspot is defined quantitatively: "a news story that has political undertones and is disproportionately covered by media sources on one side of the political spectrum"; e.g. a blindspot *for the left* requires "Fewer than 10 Left-leaning sources reporting" and "Right-leaning coverage greater than or equal to 33%." — [Ground News Rating System](https://ground.news/rating-system)
- UI: each story carries a "bias distribution" bar giving the percentage of covering outlets in left/center/right — CJR's example: a Florida vaccine-mandate story showed 55% center (e.g. CNBC), 37% left (AP), 5% right (NY Post). Founder/CEO Harleen Kaur (ex-aerospace engineer); aggregates 40,000+ outlets, ~30,000 AI summaries/day; ~8M visits in July 2025 (Similarweb); #1 free news app on Apple in spring 2025. — [CJR, "The business of balance"](https://www.cjr.org/analysis/the-business-of-balance-ground-news.php)
- Critique: AllSides' COO says Ground News uses AllSides ratings without "formal permission" or compensation; MBFC's founder confirms no agreement; only Ad Fontes is paid. CJR's core objection: the product conflates distance-from-other-coverage with truth — "truth is not a measure of how far a story strays from other coverage; it's a measure of how near it comes to reality." Kathleen Hall Jamieson: people who say they want unbiased news actually "want news consistent with [their] own biases." — [CJR](https://www.cjr.org/analysis/the-business-of-balance-ground-news.php)

**Ad Fontes Media (the taxonomy Ground News averages in)**
- Two continuous axes rather than buckets: bias from −42 (Most Extreme Left) to +42 (Most Extreme Right); reliability 0–64. Each article/episode is rated by three analysts — one right, one left, one center. — [4As summary](https://www.aaaa.org/blog/ad-fontes-media-bias-chart/); [Ad Fontes methodology paper](https://adfontesmedia.com/methodology-white-paper/)
- Poynter: seven bias markers and eight reliability markers ("Original Fact Reporting" down to "Contains Inaccurate/Fabricated Info"), ~30 analysts; Vanessa Otero: "Some nuance has to go away when it's a graphic." Kelly McBride: "Overreliance on a chart like this is going to probably give some consumers a false level of faith." — [Poynter](https://www.poynter.org/fact-checking/media-literacy/2021/should-you-trust-media-bias-charts/)
- Cross-rater agreement: a search snippet cites a Pearson correlation of 0.8 between AllSides and Ad Fontes ideology scores, with Daily Signal, Mic and NewsMax classified inconsistently; I could not locate the paper (the arXiv abstract at 2404.15631 uses AllSides labels but does not report the comparison in its abstract). Treat the 0.8 figure as unverified. — [search result, Factually.co](https://factually.co/fact-checks/media/allsides-ad-fontes-ground-news-bias-rating-methods-compared-788ca3); [arXiv 2404.15631 abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.15631)

**ProCon.org (2004– ; Britannica since May 2020)**
- Founded July 12, 2004 by Steven C. Markoff; Kamy Akhavan managing editor from Dec 2004, president 2010; acquired by Encyclopædia Britannica May 2020. Each issue page is split into "Pro" and "Con" columns of "quoted arguments of different experts"; 49 topics as of Nov 2013. Experts were ranked with a self-defined "Theoretical Expertise" one-to-five-star system. ALA "Top 25 Free Reference Websites of 2011." — [Wikipedia: ProCon.org](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ProCon.org) (britannica.com/procon/About-ProCon returned 403.)
- Wikidebates' comparison classifies ProCon as a "two-column table" format, active since 2004, and credits it (with Hyperdébat) for "rich citations and quotations." — [Wikidebates: Comparison of Existing Debate Encyclopedias](https://en.wikidebates.org/wiki/Project:Comparison_of_Existing_Debate_Encyclopedias)
- Labels are assigned by ProCon's editors to *arguments* (Pro n / Con n), not to sources' general leanings — the only product here where the label attaches to the claim rather than to the outlet.

**The Flip Side (2017– )**
- Founded 2017 by Annafi Wahed after the 2016 election; a Pittsburgh-based team "comb[s] through dozens of news sources – liberal, conservative and centrist" to produce, every weekday, an edition that "curates, in adjacent columns, commentary around a single topic from leading voices on the right and the left." 200,000+ subscribers by 2024; a premium tier with a forum had 5,000 paid. — [Fast Company](https://www.fastcompany.com/91162993/flip-side-newsletter-bipartisanship); [Technical.ly](https://technical.ly/startups/bipartisan-news-startup-2024-election/)
- Its own site: 5-minute daily digest, one topic per day, "the best points from liberal and conservative media," biweekly "Deep Dive," a Forum ranked by "a proprietary ranking algorithm" to "reward thoughtfulness and bipartisanship." **No methodology for classifying a source as left or right is disclosed.** — [theflipside.io](https://www.theflipside.io/)
- (The exact section titles "From the Left"/"From the Right" were not confirmed by any fetched page; the sources say "adjacent columns.")

**Tangle (Isaac Saul)**
- One text per issue with fixed per-perspective sections: "Today's topic" (facts, neutral), "What the left/right/center are saying" ("three arguments from each side"), "My take," "Staff dissent," "Under the radar," "Numbers," "Have a nice day." 500,000+ readers in 60+ countries; audience ~40% liberal, 30% conservative. **No stated criteria for what counts as left or right**; the site says only that it looks for "the best arguments." — [Tangle: About](https://www.readtangle.com/about/)

**Improve the News → Verity (Max Tegmark, 2020– )**
- Improve the News Foundation founded Oct 2020 by MIT's Max Tegmark and Tufts' Meia Chita-Tegmark; 501(c)(3); product renamed Verity; 5,000+ sources, 15,000+ stories, 1M+ monthly newsletters (Mar 2025); 2025 Shorty Award; AllSides Balanced Media Certification. — [Verity: About](https://www.improvethenews.org/about)
- Two sliders: political stance (left↔right) and establishment stance (pro↔critical). — [JEA](https://jea.org/digital-media/use-verity-to-help-teach-media-literacy-skills/); [Verity FAQ](https://www.improvethenews.org/faq)
- Per story, a **Facts** block ("where sources on opposite sides of the relevant controversy agree," or primary-sourced) is separated from **Narratives**, labeled by axis — left/right, pro-establishment/establishment-critical — and sometimes topic-specific labels such as "Anti-China"/"Pro-China." Outlets are scored by ChatGPT 4.1 on two 1–5 scales (political, establishment), "randomly reviewed by a team of humans and compared against ground truths from sources such as AllSides." "No content is purely AI-generated — it all undergoes rigorous human editing and review." — [Verity FAQ](https://www.improvethenews.org/faq)
- Design note: Verity is the one news product whose per-perspective texts are *written to be parallel* (each narrative is the "strongest and most prevailing" case on its side about the same story) rather than aggregated from whatever outlets published. — [Shorty Awards entry](https://shortyawards.com/18th/using-ai-to-rebuild-trust-in-news-empowering-journalists-not-replacing-them)

**"Pluralist"**
- The only "Pluralist" found is a right-leaning tabloid covering "the U.S. culture wars" (MBFC listing) and an unrelated Italian app "Media Pluralisti Europei." **No multi-perspective product named Pluralist was found**; treat the item in the brief as unverified. — [MBFC: Pluralist](https://mediabiasfactcheck.com/pluralist/); [Google Play: Media Pluralisti Europei](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=eu.mediapluralisti.app)

### Inferences
- Across AllSides, Ground News, Flip Side and Tangle, the label is a property of the *outlet* and the perspective text is whatever that outlet chose to write — so the "sides" rarely answer the same question. ProCon (argument-level Pro/Con on one question) and Verity (synthesized narratives per axis on one story) are the two parallel designs.
- Every product with a bucket taxonomy (5 or 7) has its own operator conceding that the buckets are a lossy projection of a spectrum (Mastrine, Otero); Ad Fontes' continuous coordinates are the only non-bucket taxonomy, and Ground News immediately re-buckets them by averaging.
- None of the news products aligns perspectives at the level of a specific claim or passage; the unit of comparison is the whole article (AllSides, Ground) or a paragraph-long quote (Flip Side, Tangle).

### Gaps
- AllSides' own methodology page and confidence levels ("Low/Medium/High confidence") could not be fetched (403); the five named methods are from memory of the site and are not cited here.
- The academic source for the 0.8 AllSides/Ad Fontes correlation was not identified.
- No product page for Verity's *topic-specific* narrative labels beyond the FAQ's "Anti-China"/"Pro-China" example.

## Key question 2: Reference works — separate signed entries per tradition, position taxonomies, and parallel-commentary tools

### Takeaway
Reference works achieve "labeled POV" by *venue*, not by tag: the Catholic Encyclopedia, Jewish Encyclopedia and Theopedia are whole works written from one declared standpoint, so the perspective label is the title page; the SEP and Routledge instead mandate neutral signed entries and reach multiple traditions by *separate entries per tradition*. The one genuinely parallel format is Zondervan's Counterpoints (same question, N signed essays, each responded to by the others); the parallel-commentary tools (BibleHub, Sefaria, Perseus, Bible Gateway) align perspectives at the *verse/segment* level but label them by author/work, never by school.

### Cited Findings

**Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy**
- Policy: "Entries should be objective/neutral analyses/surveys of the literature and offer a broad perspective of the topic rather than advocate a point of view"; "the balance of discussion in the entry is in proportion to, and reflects, the balance in the literature"; entries must present "the important arguments that have been put forward on both sides of an issue"; first-person advocacy ("as I (we) have argued") is prohibited; subject editors on the Editorial Board review for neutrality. The guidelines say nothing about multiple competing entries on one topic. — [SEP Guidelines](https://plato.stanford.edu/guidelines.html)
- Traditions get their own entries rather than a shared one: e.g. "Comparative Philosophy: Chinese and Western" (first published 2001-07-31, revised 2024-09-05) is organized by *topic* (methodological, metaphysical/epistemological, ethical commensurability) and points to separate tradition-specific entries (Buddhist ethics/metaphysics, Chinese metaphysics, Confucian virtue ethics, Daoist ethics, Neo-Confucianism). — [SEP: Comparative Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/comparphil-chiwes/)

**Routledge Encyclopedia of Philosophy**
- 10 volumes (1998, ed. Edward Craig), 2,000+ signed articles; entries surveying "a philosophical tradition or approach" keep "philosophical traditions from different countries together," and historical entries are filed under the topic, not the period. Entries are signed with reading suggestions. — [REP Help](https://www.rep.routledge.com/help); [Wikipedia: REP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Routledge_Encyclopedia_of_Philosophy)
- (The Macmillan *Encyclopedia of Philosophy* 2nd ed. was not investigated — gap.)

**Confessional encyclopedias as whole-work POV labels**
- Catholic Encyclopedia (vol. 1 Mar 1907, last vol. 1913, index 1914): designed "to give its readers full and authoritative information on the entire cycle of Catholic interests, action and doctrine," written "from the Roman Catholic point of view," with *nihil obstat* (Remy Lafort, 1908-11-01) and *imprimatur* (Abp. John Farley); online at New Advent since 1995 (transcription finished 1997). — [Wikipedia: Catholic Encyclopedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholic_Encyclopedia)
- Jewish Encyclopedia: 15,000+ articles, Funk & Wagnalls, 1901–1906; online at jewishencyclopedia.com. — [Wikipedia: The Jewish Encyclopedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Jewish_Encyclopedia); [jewishencyclopedia.com](https://www.jewishencyclopedia.com/)
- Theopedia (2004– ): "a conservative Christian wiki encyclopedia guided by a Reformed theology perspective," which "locates itself within the Reformed theology tradition and provides an explanation of this position, along with a statement of faith." — [Oxford ARCH record](http://arch.oucs.ox.ac.uk/detail/20532/index.html); [Christianity Knowledge Base list of Christian wikis](https://christianity.fandom.com/wiki/Christian_wikis)
- Presentation consequence: comparing "the Catholic view" and "the Jewish view" of a topic means opening two different works in two tabs — no tool aligns them, and article boundaries differ between works. (Inference; no site found that juxtaposes them.)
- Islamic/Buddhist equivalents: not investigated (gap).

**PhilPapers Survey (Bourget & Chalmers) as a position taxonomy**
- 2009 survey: 30 questions; 2020: those 30 plus 70 more (100 main questions), ~2,000 respondents; respondents first answered 50 then optionally the other 50. — [Daily Nous](https://dailynous.com/2020/10/16/2020-philpapers-survey-begins/); [PhilArchive record](https://philarchive.org/rec/BOUPOP-3)
- Question format is "Topic: option A, option B, (option C)?" with a fixed answer vocabulary — Accept / Lean toward / Reject a listed option, plus "other" responses (agnostic/undecided, question too unclear, no fact of the matter, accept a combination); in 2020 respondents could endorse multiple options. Target group ~1,700+; example results: moral realism 62%, compatibilism 62%, theism 19%. — [LessWrong summary of 2020 results](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/JTekFwz3rWjsirBbr/2020-philpapers-survey-results) (the survey site itself returned 403; percentages are as relayed by this summary)
- Published as "Philosophers on Philosophy: The 2020 PhilPapers Survey," *Philosophers' Imprint* 23(11), 2023. — [Philosophers' Imprint](https://journals.publishing.umich.edu/phimp/article/2109/galley/2463/view/)
- Relevance: it is the closest thing to a *canonical* list of named positions per philosophical question (e.g. Free will: compatibilism / libertarianism / no free will), i.e. a ready-made column set for a rows = questions, columns = positions table.

**Zondervan Counterpoints series (the parallel format)**
- "Three to five people make their case for their particular view"; "after each author makes their proposal, the others make short critical responses, and the original author then has a chance to briefly respond." Series editor Stanley N. Gundry; 43 volumes in the Logos edition (31 in an earlier bundle). Each essay is a *separate signed text* on the *same* question, and the labels are the view names in the book's table of contents (e.g. Four Views on Heaven). — [Accordance on Counterpoints](https://www.accordancebible.com/article/counterpoints-series/); [Logos: 43 vols](https://www.logos.com/product/259817/zondervan-counterpoints-series); [Monday Morning Theologian](https://mondaymorningtheologian.com/2016/03/17/zondervan-counterpoint-series/)

**Parallel commentary and translation tools (alignment at the verse/segment)**
- BibleHub: one page per verse stacks ~30 commentaries vertically, each under a bold header naming the *work* (John 1:1 lists Alford, Barnes, Bengel, Benson, Calvin, Cambridge, Chrysostom, Clarke, Darby, Ellicott, Expositor's, Geneva, Gill, Guzik, Haydock, JFB, Lange, MacLaren, Matthew Henry, Meyer, Poole, Pulpit, Scofield, Vincent, Wesley…), with a "Jump to" menu and prev/next verse arrows. **No denominational/tradition label is shown** — the reader must know that Haydock is Catholic and Calvin Reformed. — [BibleHub John 1:1 commentaries](https://biblehub.com/commentaries/john/1-1.htm); its commentaries index splits "Expository" (English-only) from "Exegetical" (original-language). — [BibleHub commentaries](https://biblehub.com/commentaries/)
- Bible Gateway: "Add parallel version" opens a new column; up to five translations side by side, verse-aligned, version abbreviation at the top of each column. — [Learn of Christ review](https://learnofchrist.com/resources/bible-gateway); [Mark Jerde walkthrough](https://markjerde.wordpress.com/2023/03/20/parallel-bibles-in-biblegateway/)
- Sefaria (founded 2011 by Joshua Foer and Brett Lockspeiser, incorporated 2013): "clicking on a verse in Tanakh will open a window on the side, allowing the user to open a commentary on that verse"; the Resource Panel's "RELATED TEXTS" section lists texts "that reference the primary text, organized by category," filterable by relevance, chronology, category and book; 775,000 monthly users in 2024, 234 countries; 12 apps used its API by 2015. — [Wikipedia: Sefaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sefaria); [Sefaria Help: Resource Panel](https://help.sefaria.org/hc/en-us/articles/18472472138652-Quick-Guide-Meet-the-Sefaria-Library-Resource-Panel) (help-center pages returned 403 to the fetcher; description rests on search snippets and Wikipedia)
- Perseus (Scaife Viewer): "add a parallel version/translation" alongside the passage; "up to seven windows side-by-side"; CHS commentaries appear as "lineated notes" on the Iliad; the Open Commentary Platform is integrated into Perseus 5/6. — [SCS review of Scaife Viewer](https://classicalstudies.org/scs-blog/stephensansom/review-perseus-digital-library-scaife-viewer); [Perseus "Beyond Translation"](https://pdldatajournal.pubpub.org/pub/el65xygp/release/1)

### Inferences
- In every parallel-commentary tool the label is the *author or work*, and the tradition is implicit; the anchoring unit is the canonical verse/segment, which is what makes alignment cheap (both sides already cite the same coordinate system). A publishing tool without a canonical segmentation has to manufacture one (quote anchors).
- Counterpoints is the only format found that combines (a) same question, (b) separate signed essays, (c) explicit view labels, and (d) *cross-responses* — i.e. the perspectives address each other, not just the reader.
- Confessional encyclopedias show that "POV label" can be a property of the *publication* rather than the *article*; a multi-POV article system is in effect hosting several such publications under one topic key.

### Gaps
- Macmillan Encyclopedia of Philosophy, Brill's Encyclopaedia of Islam, and Buddhist encyclopedias were not checked for multi-entry practice.
- No fetched source describes Sefaria's per-commentator counts or multi-panel "open in new panel" behavior in detail (403s).
- The exact PhilPapers answer vocabulary is relayed via a secondary summary; the survey site and PhilPapers record were 403.

## Key question 3: Wiki-like software with explicit perspective tagging

### Takeaway
No MediaWiki/Semantic MediaWiki extension for POV-labeled sections was found; the verified wiki precedent is Wikinfo's "sympathetic point of view" (one advocate-voiced main article per subject, dissent as *separate linked articles*), which is exactly what Wikipedia's content-forking policy forbids. The structured-debate wikis (Kialo, Debatepedia, Wikidebates) label *arguments* Pro/Con rather than tagging schools of thought, and most of the two-column ones are dead.

### Cited Findings
- Wikinfo: launched July 2003 by Fred Bauder as a Wikipedia fork; "the main article on each subject in Wikinfo adopts a 'sympathetic point of view', with other points of view usually added as articles linked to the main article"; editors are encouraged "to write additional articles if they disagree with the point of view of another article." — [Academic Kids: Wikinfo](https://academickids.com/encyclopedia/index.php/Wikinfo); [Wikibooks, How Wikipedia Works ch. 14](https://en.wikibooks.org/wiki/How_Wikipedia_Works/Chapter_14)
- Wikinfo's own policy text: "the main article on a person or institution should present the subject in a positive light, (sympathetic point of view); critical or alternative viewpoints should also be presented, but not necessarily in the main article on the subject." No naming convention for dissent articles is given; current activity status not stated. — [Wikinfo About](https://www.wikinfo.org/w/index-php/wikinfo_about.html)
- Wikipedia's rule it forked from: a POV fork is "a content fork deliberately created to avoid a neutral point of view"; prohibited because "all facts and major points of view on a certain subject should be treated in one article." Allowed alternatives: summary-style spinouts; "Criticism of" articles only if they present "both the merits and faults"; and articles whose *subject* is a viewpoint (Evolution vs. Creationism) "provided titles clearly indicate the subject's nature, content remains neutral, and cross-references link related perspectives." — [Wikipedia:Content forking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Content_forking)
- Semantic MediaWiki: "richly structured wikis with multiple perspectives" appears among *envisaged* applications, but no extension dedicated to point-of-view labeling was found in the extension registry or search. — [Meta: SMW Envisaged applications](https://meta.wikimedia.org/wiki/Semantic_MediaWiki/Envisaged_applications); [SMW extensions list](https://www.semantic-mediawiki.org/wiki/Help:Semantic_MediaWiki_extensions)
- "Politicalcompass wiki": nothing verifiable found; omitted.
- Debate encyclopedias (Wikidebates' comparison of 13): structure — tree/hierarchical: Kialo (2017), Riyarchy, Argüman (2015), DebateGraph (2008); two-column: Debatepedia (2007, read-only since Nov 2011), ProCon (2004), Idebate (2011), Debatewise (2007, "no longer updated"); list: Hyperdébat (2002, read-only since Jan 2017), Argumentrix (2011, inactive since 2013); box-embedded: Wikidebate (2016); card: Proversi (2015). What worked: "recursive argument levels (Kialo, Riyarchy)," "rich citations and quotations (Hyperdébat, ProCon)," open licenses. What failed: "shallow argument depth," weak citations, "lack of interlinking between debates," copyright, "skeletal interfaces." — [Wikidebates comparison](https://en.wikidebates.org/wiki/Project:Comparison_of_Existing_Debate_Encyclopedias)
- Kialo: thesis at the top; pro (green) and con (red) claims underneath, each of which can be attacked or supported in turn; new claims are moderator-approved; users rate impact on the parent claim. — [Kialo on Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kialo); [ResearchGate figure of a Kialo tree](https://www.researchgate.net/figure/An-example-of-a-path-through-a-Kialo-debate-tree-Green-boxes-indicate-arguments-that_fig1_346555272)

### Inferences
- The only wiki that labeled *whole articles* by stance (Wikinfo) labeled them by *attitude to the subject* (sympathetic vs critical), not by school; the school-of-thought label was carried, if at all, by the article title.
- Kialo's label vocabulary is relational (pro/con *of the parent claim*), which is a different axis from "which tradition says this" and cannot express "Reformed and Catholic both say X, for different reasons."

### Gaps
- Whether Wikinfo is still online/editable in 2026 was not determined.
- No MediaWiki extension for per-section POV labels was found; absence of evidence, not proof.

## Key question 4: Comparative-religion / comparative-philosophy tools — table structure

### Takeaway
The comparison-chart genre is uniformly rows = attributes/questions, columns = traditions, cells = short phrases, authored by one (usually non-affiliated) editor; the Wikipedia "differences between X and Y" genre is instead topic-sectioned *prose* with no table, and Patheos' Side by Side is the one interactive version (pick up to three traditions, click a section to drill down).

### Cited Findings
- ReligionFacts "Christianity, Islam and Judaism" chart: three columns; ~30 rows — etymology, adherents called, date/place founded, languages, founders, expansion, schisms, branches, main locations, adherents (world/USA/Canada/UK), texts, creeds, religious law, religious authority, doctrine of scripture, prophets, god(s), spiritual beings, revered humans, messiah, Jesus (birth/death/resurrection/second coming), revelation, human nature, salvation, predestination, afterlife, house of worship, day of worship, clergy, practices, holidays, symbols. Author: ReligionFacts, "no affiliation to any religion." Also a "Big Religion Chart." — [ReligionFacts chart](https://religionfacts.com/charts/christianity-islam-judaism); [Big Religion Chart](https://religionfacts.com/big-religion-chart)
- Rose Publishing "Denominations Comparison": 12 columns (Catholic, Orthodox, Lutheran, Anglican, Presbyterian, Methodist, Anabaptist, Congregational, Baptist, Churches of Christ, Adventist, Pentecostal) × 11 rows (founding, adherents in 2000, Scripture, God, Jesus, salvation, after death, Church, sacraments, other practices, divisions/trends); columns ordered chronologically, liturgical before non-liturgical; a 33-inch fold-out. — [Amazon listing](https://www.amazon.com/Denominations-Comparison-Rose-Publishing/dp/1890947350); [Rose PDF sample](https://socialtheology.com/docs/Rose_Bible_eCharts_DC_Family_Tree_of_Denominations.pdf)
- Patheos "Side by Side Comparison Lens": "compare up to three differing religious traditions," chosen from drop-downs, with clickable major sections for "more detailed comparisons"; Patheos launched May 2009 with a library covering 27 religions/worldviews. — [Patheos Side by Side](https://www.patheos.com/library/lenses/side-by-side); [Wikipedia: Patheos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patheos)
- christianityinview.com hosts "Comparison between Orthodoxy, Protestantism & Roman Catholicism" (three-column) — the site failed TLS on fetch; structure unverified beyond the title. — [christianityinview.com](http://christianityinview.com/comparison.html)
- Wikipedia genre: "Theological differences between the Catholic Church and the Eastern Orthodox Church" is prose in topic sections (Areas of doctrinal agreement; East–West schism; Papal primacy; Filioque; Sacraments; Neo-Palamism; Future directions), each section giving one side's view then the other's; **no comparison table**; the "agreement" section is a bullet list. ### Inferences
- Charts are the *most* parallel format (every column answers every row) and the *least* attributable (one anonymous editor writes all columns; no tradition speaks for itself). Counterpoints and the confessional encyclopedias are the reverse.
- Wikipedia's prose-differences articles are parallel in intent but the alignment is only as fine as a section heading; a reader cannot select "the Filioque claim" and see both sides' *own* wording.

### Gaps
- No site was found where each column of a comparison chart is *written by* an adherent of that column's tradition (the "Compare Beliefs" pattern in the brief). Not verified to exist.

## Key question 5: Design lessons — parallel vs. aggregated, and claim-level alignment

### Takeaway
Only four of the surveyed designs make perspectives answer the *same* question — ProCon, Verity's narratives, Counterpoints, and the comparison charts; the rest aggregate what each source happened to say. Claim- or passage-level alignment exists only where a canonical coordinate system pre-exists (verse numbers on BibleHub/Sefaria/Perseus, a thesis node in Kialo); no news or encyclopedic product does it.

### Cited Findings
- Parallel-by-construction: ProCon's Pro/Con columns on one question — [Wikipedia: ProCon.org](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ProCon.org); Verity's per-story Facts + labeled Narratives — [Verity FAQ](https://www.improvethenews.org/faq); Counterpoints' essays plus cross-responses and rejoinders — [Accordance](https://www.accordancebible.com/article/counterpoints-series/); ReligionFacts/Rose rows — [ReligionFacts](https://religionfacts.com/charts/christianity-islam-judaism).
- Aggregated: AllSides and Ground News columns are whole articles from differently rated outlets — [AllSides search](https://www.allsides.com/search), [Ground News rating system](https://ground.news/rating-system); Flip Side and Tangle quote whichever commentators the editors picked — [Fast Company](https://www.fastcompany.com/91162993/flip-side-newsletter-bipartisanship), [Tangle About](https://www.readtangle.com/about/).
- Passage-level alignment: BibleHub (one page per verse, ~30 commentaries) — [BibleHub](https://biblehub.com/commentaries/john/1-1.htm); Sefaria (click a segment → sidebar of linked texts by category) — [Wikipedia: Sefaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sefaria); Perseus (lineated commentary notes, up to seven parallel windows) — [SCS review](https://classicalstudies.org/scs-blog/stephensansom/review-perseus-digital-library-scaife-viewer); Bible Gateway (five verse-aligned columns) — [Learn of Christ](https://learnofchrist.com/resources/bible-gateway).
- Claim-level alignment: Kialo's tree attaches every pro/con to a specific parent claim — [Kialo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kialo).
- What killed the two-column debate wikis: shallow depth, weak citation, no interlinking, closed licenses (Debatepedia read-only 2011, Debatewise dead, Argumentrix dead 2013). — [Wikidebates comparison](https://en.wikidebates.org/wiki/Project:Comparison_of_Existing_Debate_Encyclopedias)
- Taxonomy honesty: 5/7-bucket labels are conceded lossy by their own operators — [Poynter](https://www.poynter.org/fact-checking/media-literacy/2021/should-you-trust-media-bias-charts/); Ground News averages three raters with different methods into one bucket — [Ground News](https://ground.news/rating-system); CJR: agreement-across-outlets is not truth — [CJR](https://www.cjr.org/analysis/the-business-of-balance-ground-news.php).

### Inferences (for a publishing tool adding labeled POVs to one article)
- Label the *text*, not the *author's outlet*: the products readers trust for comparison (ProCon, Counterpoints, charts) put the label on the argument; the outlet-label products are the ones under methodological attack.
- Two viable shapes exist and they are different products: (a) one shared article with per-perspective sections (Tangle, Verity, Wikipedia differences pages) — cheap alignment, one editorial voice owns the frame; (b) separate signed texts per perspective on one topic key (Counterpoints, Wikinfo, the confessional encyclopedias) — authentic voice, but alignment must be added (Counterpoints does it socially via responses; Sefaria/BibleHub do it via a shared segment index).
- Passage-level comparison needs a shared coordinate: verse numbers in the Bible tools, the claim node in Kialo. For free-form articles the analog is a quote anchor into the shared base text — which is what lets a reader ask "what does the Reformed section say about *this sentence* of the Catholic section."
- A fixed taxonomy is less important than a *declared* one: PhilPapers' per-question option lists, Rose's 12 named denominations and Verity's per-story axis labels all work because the reader can see the whole label set at once; AllSides/Ground's failure mode is a label whose derivation is hidden (averaged third-party ratings).

### Gaps
- No academic usability study comparing parallel vs. aggregated presentations was found in this pass.
- No product was found that lets a reader align two *schools'* texts at the sentence level outside of scripture tools.
