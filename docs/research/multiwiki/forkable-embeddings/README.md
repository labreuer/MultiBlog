# Precedents for forkable embeddings

**Question.** Verbatim; the paths are as they were at the time.

> Read docs/research/MultiWiki/main_article.md & docs/research/MultiWiki/overview.md . One of the
> main messages I got from that is that forking was too easy and comparing was too hard. But I'm
> also thinking that plenty of forking needs to happen at a multi-article level, and sometimes at
> a sub-article level. Coordinate systems can be grown into existence later, perhaps by
> ontologies & translations between ontologies. My present idea is to introduce the notion of an
> 'embedding', which works like this:
>
> 1. whole entities (docs and others which haven't been created yet, like timelines) or parts of
>    entities can be assigned to one or more embeddings
> 2. relationships between entities can also be assigned to an embedding
> 3. embeddings can fork from each other
> 4. it will be useful to note the ydoc_update_id or other revision # of entities when they are
>    added to an embedding
>    * and possibly re-affirm updated versions still fit in that embedding
>
> This could ostensibly service various purposes:
>
> * compare & contrast schools of thought in a discipline
> * compare & contrast different tellings of some history
> * compare & contrast different theoretical framings
> * document the changes in a discipline within a historical trajectory (including Kuhnian
>   paradigm shifts)
>
> Look for any scholarship which might match what I'm trying to do, including the CSCW
> literature. Then, read /home/labreuer/Downloads/claude/WJWR-thin-language-handoff.md and
> comment on whether this may be a route out of the situation MacIntyre describes: "the confident
> belief that all cultural phenomena must be potentially translucent to understanding, that all
> texts must be capable of being translated into the language which the adherents of modernity
> speak to each other".

Two follow-ups came in the same session: read chapters 17–20 of *Whose Justice? Which
Rationality?* and correct anything missed, then find a better term than "embedding". Both are
