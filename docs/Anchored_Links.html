Anchored Links

An anchored link is a URL for a set of passages — one or more selections gathered across docs and PDFs, collected in a cross-page tray and minted as a single ?sel= hyperlink. Every mock below is drawn in the app's own globals.css tokens, so this page previews correctly in light and dark. Violet badges are spec annotations, not proposed chrome.

select a passage→ Add to link→ repeat, any doc or PDF→ Copy link→ share URL→ follow: highlights + banner
Fig. 1 — Creating: first passage /doc/[slug], reading view

The existing selection popover grows one button. Clicking Add to link posts the part to the server immediately (captured against its own version stamp) and clears the selection; the tray appears with the first passage.

Quotation and Context

Luke Breuer · 8/29/2026 hermeneutics

A quotation is a promise that the words appeared, and an implicit claim about what they meant where they appeared. The first half is cheap to verify; the second is where nearly every dispute about quotation actually lives.

To cite a passage honestly, then, is to cite its surroundings — the sentence that a quotation was lifted from remains the best commentary on the quotation itself, and any apparatus that carries the one without the other invites the reader to supply context from imagination.

The practical question is mechanical: how does a reference keep pointing at a passage while the passage's document continues to change around it?

It is precisely here that the history of a word becomes indispensable. The senses do not replace one another cleanly, like sentries changing guard; the old sense lives on beside the new, and colours it, and is coloured by it in turn. A reader who knows only the newest sense will not exactly misread the older authors — he will read them smoothly, confidently, and wrongly.

The dictionary can warn us of this, but only the habit of attending to whole passages can cure it. Words, like men, are known by the company they keep.

Fig. 5 — Following /pdf/[slug]?sel=…

On the PDF surface the same banner rides as an overlay, the viewer scrolls to the first passage's page, and each passage is drawn as an outline in the accent — an outline rather than a fill so it composes over annotation fills, and it takes no clicks, so annotations underneath stay clickable.

Fig. 6 — Visibility filtered per target

A link referencing a passage in a private doc plus one in a shared PDF, viewed on the PDF's page. The banner and this page's highlights render for every viewer who can read this page — but each target group appears only for viewers who may read that target. An unreadable group is silently omitted: no “N hidden passages”, no placeholder, nothing to distinguish “there's more I can't see” from “there's nothing else.”

Viewer who can read both targets
Banner shows this page's passage and the “Also referenced” row pointing into the private doc.
Viewer denied the private doc
Same banner, same highlights — the private doc's group is simply absent. Its own page still forbids them as it always did.

Deliberately not in this sketch

  • No painting of draft parts while collecting — the tray's text list is the only feedback until the link is followed.
  • No editing after mint, no management surface (/links table is a follow-up), no link labels.
  • Multi-page PDF selections anchor to their start page (existing capture limitation, kept).
  • Post pages and annotation bodies are not linkable targets in v1.
